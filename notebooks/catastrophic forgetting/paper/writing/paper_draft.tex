\begin{abstract}
% ===========================================================================
The literature ships a new parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapter almost
every week, each claiming to mitigate catastrophic forgetting through some clever
geometric prior---a data-aware initialization, a null-space projection, a spectral
decomposition, an orthogonality penalty. We report a controlled study of eight
adapters (plain LoRA, LoRA{+}weight-decay, DoRA, CorDA, MiLoRA, SC-LoRA, LoRA-Null,
and CLoRA), of which we can currently assess six fairly on the headline
curve---CorDA is withheld pending a calibration-fairness fix and LoRA-Null is presently
pooled into LoRA (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); two task domains
(commonsense, math), and, as a partial replication, Qwen2.5-7B
($\sim$13 of $\sim$112 planned cells: the commonsense LoRA sweep replicates the sign of
the law, math-LoRA does not yet) on top of the complete Llama-2-7B study, sweeping seven
learning rates per method. Our central finding is a \emph{mechanism}: forgetting is
governed by a single scalar, the token-weighted Frobenius norm of the weight update
$\dw$, and \emph{not} by the geometric structure of the adapter that produced it.
Retention collapses onto one curve set by $\dw$
($r\!=\!-0.86$ pooled, $-0.92$ on the well-behaved adapters; slope
$\approx\!-10$ to $-15$ percentage points per decade of $\dw$), and an ANCOVA shows
that adapter identity adds essentially nothing once $\dw$ is controlled (five of six
adapters are statistically indistinguishable from the pooled curve). The
\emph{consequence} is that the simplest possible magnitude control---plain LoRA with a
weight-decay term---lands on the adaptation-versus-retention Pareto frontier of
every elaborate geometric adapter, matching or edging it, because weight decay is the
most direct way to bound $\dw$. The \emph{diagnosis} is that we can exhibit the
\emph{ingredients} of a learning-rate artifact behind the fancy adapters' reported wins:
each method peaks at a different learning rate, so a single fixed learning rate flatters
whichever adapter happens to be well tuned there, and the $\dw$ an adapter produces
predicts retention far better than its learning rate does---so a per-method sweep
collapses the differences back onto the shared magnitude curve. We make this concrete:
at a single fixed learning rate several adapters beat plain LoRA (SC-LoRA by $+26$\,pp of
adaptation, DoRA by $+6$), yet the swept LoRA{+}wd frontier dominates every one of those
best single-LR points on both axes (Figure~\ref{fig:lrartifact}). (Reproducing a
specific \emph{published} win from a fixed-LR baseline and dissolving it, plus
seed-averaging, is deferred; see Section~\ref{sec:lr}.) Learning rate
predicts retention only loosely ($R^2\!=\!0.32$) whereas
the $\dw$ it produces predicts it tightly ($R^2\!=\!0.74$). The \emph{message} is a
wake-up call to a fast-moving field: control the magnitude, not the geometry.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================
Catastrophic forgetting---the erosion of a pretrained model's general capabilities when
it is fine-tuned on a narrow task---is one of the central obstacles to deploying
adapted large language models. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), and in
particular LoRA and its many descendants, was supposed to help: by restricting the
update to a low-rank correction, one hopes to leave the bulk of the pretrained weights
untouched. The community has responded to residual forgetting with a steady stream of
increasingly elaborate \emph{geometric} adapters. Some initialize the adapter from the
principal or minor singular directions of the pretrained weight
(PiSSA, MiLoRA); some from a data- or activation-covariance decomposition that
identifies and freezes the ``knowledge'' subspace (CorDA, SC-LoRA); some project the
update into an input null-space (LoRA-Null); some add an orthogonality penalty against a
random subspace (CLoRA); some decompose magnitude from direction (DoRA). Each is
presented as a better way to \emph{choose where the update goes}, and each reports a win
over the last.

This paper is a controlled, deliberately negative, wake-up-call contribution. We show
that those wins are largely an artifact, and that a single scalar---the magnitude of the
weight update, $\dw$---governs forgetting in a way that is essentially
\emph{method-independent}. Our argument has four parts, which form the spine of the paper:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Mechanism.} Forgetting is governed by $\dw$, not by adapter geometry.
        Across the six of eight adapters we can currently assess fairly (LoRA, LoRA{+}wd,
        DoRA, MiLoRA, CLoRA, LoRA-Null), two domains, and a partial second model, retention
        falls onto one curve set by $\dw$; SC-LoRA is the one provisional below-curve
        deviator and CorDA is withheld (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). We take care never to
        put ``eight adapters'' next to ``one curve'' (Section~\ref{sec:law}).
  \item \textbf{Consequence.} Plain LoRA{+}weight-decay lands on---matching or
        edging---the adaptation--retention Pareto frontier of every elaborate geometric
        adapter, because weight decay is the simplest way to bound $\dw$
        (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}).
  \item \textbf{Diagnosis.} We exhibit the \emph{ingredients} of a learning-rate/magnitude
        artifact behind the fancy adapters' reported wins: the best learning rate is not
        shared across methods, and the $\dw$ an adapter produces predicts retention far
        better than its learning rate does. Evaluated at a single learning rate a method
        can look better; a per-method sweep collapses the methods onto the shared
        magnitude curve. The learning-rate-sweep-per-method is the instrument that
        exposes this; reproducing and then dissolving a specific published win is deferred
        (Section~\ref{sec:lr}).
  \item \textbf{Message.} Control the magnitude, not the geometry.
\end{enumerate}

We state our scope honestly up front. The Llama-2-7B evidence is mature: it comprises
the full $8\times 7$ learning-rate grid on commonsense and a sparser grid on math, on a
single seed (seed~42). The Qwen2.5-7B arm is a replication \emph{in progress}
($\sim$13 of $\sim$112 planned cells, mostly the LoRA arm); we present it as supporting
evidence, not as a completed second model. Section~\ref{sec:limits} is unflinching about
these gaps---single seed, unmatched ranks, the calibration-versus-evaluation mismatch
for the data-aware inits, and the near-tautological character of the raw magnitude
correlation---because owning them is the only honest way to make the argument.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}\label{sec:related}
% ===========================================================================
\paragraph{The adapter zoo.} LoRA~\citep{hu2021lora} reparameterizes a weight update as
a low-rank product $\Delta W = BA$. The subsequent literature can be organized by the
\emph{basis} in which it reasons about which directions to protect. One family works in
the \emph{static weight-SVD} basis: PiSSA initializes from the top singular triples of
$W_0$, MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora} from the bottom (minor) triples, and OPLoRA and
related methods project updates orthogonally to the dominant weight subspace.
%TODO(data): verify arXiv IDs before submission (OPLoRA 2510.13003, CorDA 2406.05223,
%SC-LoRA 2505.23724 / OpenReview KAE9YDK0t8, CorDA++ 2506.13187, DoRA); 2603.02224 is
%almost certainly wrong.
A second family works in the \emph{data / activation-covariance} basis: CorDA
\citep{yang2024corda} takes the SVD of $W_0\,\mathrm{Cov}_{\text{input}}$ over a
calibration set and freezes the components most responsive to that data, and SC-LoRA
constructs a subspace from a difference of task and preservation covariances. A third
family constrains the update via an auxiliary loss: CLoRA~\citep{clora} penalizes the
overlap of the adapter with a frozen random subspace. DoRA~\citep{liu2024dora}
decomposes each column of the update into magnitude and direction. What all of these
share is a commitment to \emph{geometry}: the belief that \emph{where} the update lands
is the lever that controls forgetting.

\paragraph{Why magnitude is overlooked.} Weight decay is the oldest, simplest knob in
deep learning, and it acts directly on the \emph{size} of the update rather than its
direction. Precisely because it carries no geometric story, it is almost never the
protagonist of an adapter paper. The one prior work that explicitly connects update
magnitude to forgetting is CLoRA, whose penalty can be read as an indirect magnitude
control. Our contribution is therefore \emph{not} the discovery that magnitude matters.
It is (i) that magnitude is the \emph{method-free} governing variable---the same
retention-versus-$\dw$ curve holds across the structurally distinct adapters we test
(the six of eight we can currently assess fairly), two domains, and a partial second
model; (ii) that once $\dw$ is controlled, geometry
is causally close to inert (Section~\ref{sec:law}); and (iii) that per-method wins
reported in the literature are largely a learning-rate artifact that a per-method sweep
dissolves (Section~\ref{sec:lr}). Even if one accepts CLoRA's magnitude--forgetting
link, our delta is the demonstration of method-freeness and the diagnosis of the
artifact.

\section{Setup and Measurement}\label{sec:setup}
% ===========================================================================
\paragraph{Models and domains.} Our primary, complete evidence is on
\textbf{Llama-2-7B}; a replication on \textbf{Qwen2.5-7B} is in progress. We fine-tune
on two domains: \emph{commonsense} (the eight-task LLM-Adapters commonsense suite) and
\emph{math} (trained on MetaMathQA, evaluated on GSM8K).

\paragraph{The adapters, as one trainer.} A key design decision makes the comparison
fair: every structured adapter is implemented as ``LoRA with a different
initialization'' loaded into a single shared PEFT LoRA trainer, so that the only
difference between arms is the init routine---extracted as $(A_{\text{init}},
B_{\text{init}}, W_{\text{residual}})$---and, for CLoRA alone, one added loss term. We
study eight arms: plain \textbf{LoRA} (r16), \textbf{LoRA{+}wd} (r32, weight decay
0.3 on the adapter matrices only), \textbf{DoRA} (r16), \textbf{CorDA} (r16, KPA
retention mode, nq\_open calibration), \textbf{MiLoRA} (r32), \textbf{SC-LoRA} (r32,
$\beta{=}0.5$), \textbf{LoRA-Null} (r16), and \textbf{CLoRA} ($k{=}1024$). Before
claiming any method ``fails,'' we audited each port against its reference
implementation; all ports are faithful, and the residual-init methods (MiLoRA, SC-LoRA)
pass a 0-step self-check that a $\Delta W = 0$ update recovers base retention. This
faithful-port claim is scoped to the six methods we report on; CorDA did \emph{not} pass
a clean self-check under the current calibration (a residual-save bug produced an exploded
update on its highest-LR cell) and is withheld pending its re-run
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Ranks are \emph{not} matched across arms; we return to this in
Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\paragraph{Learning-rate sweep.} We sweep seven learning rates per method,
$\{2\text{e-}5, 5\text{e-}5, 1\text{e-}4, 2\text{e-}4, 3\text{e-}4, 5\text{e-}4,
1\text{e-}3\}$. The two highest rates originally in the grid (2e-3, 5e-3) diverged to
NaN weights on every method and are dropped; the forgetting regime is already reached at
1e-3. The per-method sweep is the paper's central instrument
(Section~\ref{sec:lr}).

\paragraph{Retention and adaptation metrics.} \emph{Retention (core)} is the mean of
BBH (answer-only, 3-shot) and MMLU-Pro (5-shot CoT); the no-fine-tuning base ceiling is
$33.10$ and $18.96$ respectively, for a core ceiling of $\mathbf{26.0}$. \emph{Retention
(broad)} adds MMLU, ARC-Challenge, and TruthfulQA. \emph{Adaptation} is eight-task
commonsense accuracy (CS-8) or GSM8K exact-match (math).
%TODO(data): base ceilings for MMLU / ARC / TruthfulQA are not yet calibrated (5
%eval-only runs), so broad-retention percentages and their per-benchmark slopes are not
%yet normalizable.

\paragraph{The magnitude axis $\dw$.} Our governing variable is the
\textbf{token-weighted Frobenius norm of the weight update}, $\dw$ (the registry field
\texttt{fdelta}, ranging $\sim\!0.05$--$3.7$). We adopt this measure over the maximum
spectral norm $\smax$ because $\smax$ is method-confounded: spiky inits (CorDA, DoRA,
SC-LoRA) inflate it, which would mis-rank them. Figure~\ref{fig:axis} shows that among
three candidate magnitude measures ($\dw$, mean spectral norm, and $\smax$), the
token-weighted Frobenius norm gives the tightest, most method-neutral fit, so it is the
fair $x$-axis for the whole paper.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_magnitude_law.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Choosing a fair magnitude axis.} Retention versus three candidate
  magnitude measures (log $x$-axis), pooled over all adapters. Token-weighted Frobenius
  $\dw$ (highlighted panel) gives the tightest fit ($r\!=\!-0.86$, $R^2\!=\!0.74$);
  $\smax$ is loosest and method-confounded because spiky inits inflate it. We adopt
  $\dw$ as the axis for the entire study.}
  \label{fig:axis}
\end{figure}

\section{The Magnitude Law}\label{sec:law}
% ===========================================================================
Our first and load-bearing result is that retention is a tight, monotone, and
\emph{method-independent} function of $\dw$. Figure~\ref{fig:hero} is the hero exhibit:
plotting core retention against $\dw$ (log scale) for all adapters, the points from
every method interleave along one downward curve.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig0_hero.png}
  \caption{\textbf{The magnitude law.} Core retention (mean of BBH and MMLU-Pro) versus
  $\dw$ (log scale), Llama-2-7B commonsense, seed 42. Every adapter's points interleave
  along one curve. Black line: pooled fit on the on-curve adapters with a 95\% bootstrap
  CI ($r\!=\!-0.92$, $R^2\!=\!0.84$, slope $-10.0$\,pp/decade, $n\!=\!42$); the
  all-adapters pooled fit is $r\!=\!-0.86$, slope $-14.8$\,pp/decade
  ($n\!=\!49\!=\!7\text{ LR}\times 7\text{ series}$; LoRA-Null is presently pooled into
  the LoRA series, see Section~\ref{sec:limits}). SC-LoRA is ringed as the single
  below-curve deviator (provisional; confounded by the calibration-vs-evaluation
  mismatch, Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Green dotted line: base ceiling (26.0).}
  \label{fig:hero}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The correlations.} On Llama-2 commonsense, the pooled correlation between
retention and $\log_{10}\dw$ is $r\!=\!-0.86$ ($R^2\!=\!0.74$, slope
$-14.8$\,pp/decade, $n\!=\!49\!=\!7\text{ LR}\times 7\text{ series}$,
$p\!=\!3\!\times\!10^{-15}$; the 7 series are the six visible adapters plus LoRA-Null,
which is presently pooled into the LoRA series---see Section~\ref{sec:limits}); excluding
the one off-curve method (SC-LoRA) tightens it to $r\!=\!-0.92$ ($R^2\!=\!0.84$, slope
$-10.0$\,pp/decade, $n\!=\!42$). Crucially, \emph{every adapter individually} traces the
same line: within-method correlations run from $-0.86$ to $-0.97$ (plain LoRA $-0.95$,
LoRA{+}wd $-0.89$, DoRA $-0.97$, MiLoRA $-0.94$, CLoRA $-0.90$, SC-LoRA $-0.97$,
LoRA-Null $-0.86$; the plotted LoRA series, which currently pools LoRA-Null, is $-0.90$),
and the points interleave rather than forming method-specific sub-curves. (CorDA is
excluded from every within-method figure; see Section~\ref{sec:limits}.)

\paragraph{Both domains.} The law is not a commonsense idiosyncrasy: on math (GSM8K) the
pooled correlation is $r\!=\!-0.97$ ($R^2\!=\!0.93$, slope $-10.1$\,pp/decade,
$n\!=\!14$). The math arm is sparse---it currently covers only three adapters (LoRA,
LoRA{+}wd, and DoRA), and not all learning-rate cells are filled ($n\!=\!14$ pooled)---so
we present it as a genuine second domain but flag its thinness.

\paragraph{Non-circularity: geometry adds nothing.} The raw correlation ``a bigger
update forgets more'' is near-tautological, so we do not rest the claim on it. The
non-trivial statement is that \emph{method identity adds essentially nothing beyond
$\dw$}. We test this with an ANCOVA (Figure~\ref{fig:fairness}): a pooled log-linear fit
gives $R^2\!=\!0.74$; adding per-method intercepts raises it to $R^2\!=\!0.87$, an
improvement that is significant overall ($F(5,42)\!=\!8.3$, $p\!<\!0.001$) but is driven
almost entirely by a single method. The per-method residuals from the pooled curve are
$+0.79$ (LoRA; currently pooled with LoRA-Null, see Section~\ref{sec:limits}), $+0.06$
(LoRA{+}wd), $+1.04$ (MiLoRA), $+0.09$ (CLoRA), and $+1.37$
(DoRA)---all statistically indistinguishable from zero---and $-4.15$\,pp for SC-LoRA
($p\!=\!0.006$), the sole significant deviator. Five of six adapters are on the law.
We treat the SC-LoRA deviation as \emph{provisional}: it is confounded by the
calibration-versus-evaluation mismatch (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) and rests on a single
seed, so we ring it as an open question rather than headline it as a real geometric
effect.

\paragraph{The law acts per capability.} Decomposing retention into its constituent
benchmarks (Figure~\ref{fig:perbench}), every capability degrades monotonically with
$\dw$ except one. Broad factual knowledge dies fastest---MMLU at $-23.4$\,pp/decade
($r\!=\!-0.93$)---followed by MMLU-Pro ($-15.2$), ARC-Challenge ($-14.9$), and BBH
($-14.3$). TruthfulQA is essentially flat ($-0.5$\,pp/decade, $r\!=\!-0.10$): truthful
behavior appears flat with respect to update magnitude, pending a floor check (the
MMLU/ARC/TruthfulQA base ceilings are not yet calibrated, so we cannot yet rule out a
near-chance-scoring artifact). This texture---one law with a per-capability slope, and
one benchmark that resists---is evidence that the phenomenon is not an artifact of a
single metric.

\paragraph{Ruling out the confounds.} Three checks confirm that $\dw$, and not a
correlate of it, is the governing variable. (i) A LoRA rank sweep (r4 to r256) drives
retention down purely by raising $\dw$, with $\sigma_1$ growing with rank and no
independent ``rank effect''---the earlier folklore that rank mitigates forgetting folds
entirely into the magnitude law. (ii) The direction of the update in the static
weight-SVD basis, measured by a leakage thermometer $\mu_E$ (the output leak into the
preserved subspace), predicts retention at $r\!\approx\!-0.09$---i.e.\ not at all.
(iii) $\dw$ beats $\smax$ as the axis because $\smax$ is inflated by method-specific
spikiness (Figure~\ref{fig:axis}, Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\section{Learning Rate Is Only a Proxy}\label{sec:lr}
% ===========================================================================
If forgetting is governed by $\dw$, then learning rate should matter only insofar as it
sets $\dw$---and, critically, different adapters should transmit the \emph{same}
learning rate into \emph{different} $\dw$. Both hold, and together they are the
mechanism of the reported ``wins.''

\paragraph{$\dw$ beats learning rate as a predictor.} Regressing retention on
$\log(\text{learning rate})$ gives $R^2\!=\!0.32$ ($r\!=\!-0.57$); regressing it on
$\log(\dw)$ gives $R^2\!=\!0.74$ ($r\!=\!-0.86$). The variance explained more than
doubles when the $x$-axis switches from the learning rate to the update magnitude it
produces (Figure~\ref{fig:proxy}). Learning rate is a confounded proxy for the true
cause.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig7_lr_is_the_proxy.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Learning rate is only a proxy; $\dw$ is the cause.} Panel A: the same
  learning rate maps to different $\dw$ depending on the init (data-aware inits turn a
  fixed LR into a larger update). Panels B and C share a $y$-axis: retention versus
  learning rate is loose ($R^2\!=\!0.32$), retention versus $\dw$ is tight
  ($R^2\!=\!0.74$).}
  \label{fig:proxy}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The best learning rate is not shared.} Each method peaks at a different
learning rate: SC-LoRA at 5e-5, DoRA at 2e-4, LoRA and MiLoRA at 3e-4, LoRA{+}wd and
CLoRA at 5e-4 (Figure~\ref{fig:lrsens}). A single fixed learning rate therefore flatters
whichever method happens to be well tuned at that rate---which is precisely the mechanism
by which the literature's single-LR comparisons produce ``wins.'' Because a data-aware
init starts the adapter where the loss gradient is large, at a fixed learning rate it
accumulates a \emph{larger} $\dw$ than a vanilla init (Figure~\ref{fig:proxy}, panel A);
geometry does not change the \emph{shape} of the retention curve, it changes \emph{where
on the curve} a given learning rate lands you.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig4_lr_sensitivity.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Learning-rate optima are not shared.} Adaptation (left) and retention
  (right) versus learning rate; rings mark each method's adaptation-optimal rate. The
  optimum ranges over $\{5\text{e-}5, 2\text{e-}4, 3\text{e-}4, 5\text{e-}4\}$, so
  evaluating every method at one fixed learning rate biases the comparison---the root of
  the reported ``wins.''}
  \label{fig:lrsens}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The single-LR illusion, made explicit.} We can now show the illusion
directly rather than assert it (Figure~\ref{fig:lrartifact}, Table~\ref{tab:lrartifact}).
For each structured adapter we take its \emph{best-looking single learning rate}---the
rate that maximizes commonsense adaptation while keeping core retention $\geq\!24$---and
compare it against plain LoRA \emph{run at that same rate}. At that fixed rate several
methods do look better than LoRA: SC-LoRA by $+26.0$\,pp of adaptation, LoRA-Null by
$+19.5$, DoRA by $+5.9$, MiLoRA by $+0.8$ (the illusion is strong for the low-LR-optimal
data-aware inits, weak for MiLoRA, and \emph{absent} for CLoRA, which is $-14.2$ even at
its own best rate). But once the learning rate is swept, LoRA{+}wd traces a Pareto
frontier that dominates every one of these best single-LR points on \emph{both} axes
(rings in Figure~\ref{fig:lrartifact}; ``dominates? yes'' for all rows in
Table~\ref{tab:lrartifact}). The apparent per-method win is thus decomposed into a
learning-rate-tuning effect that vanishes under a symmetric sweep, plus a residual that
lands back on the shared magnitude curve. This is the concrete, per-method form of
Claim~3.

\section{The Pareto Result: LoRA{+}wd on the Frontier}\label{sec:pareto}
% ===========================================================================
The consequence of the magnitude law is practical. If $\dw$ is the true lever, the
simplest possible control of it---plain LoRA with a weight-decay term on the adapter
matrices---should reproduce or beat the frontier of the elaborate geometric adapters.

\section{Mechanism: Magnitude as a Two-Edged Budget}\label{sec:mech}
% ===========================================================================
Why does weight decay land in the right place ``for free''? Because $\dw$ is a
two-edged budget, and weight decay controls it directly. Adaptation \emph{rises} with
$\dw$ at $+20.3$\,pp/decade, while retention \emph{falls} at $-14.8$\,pp/decade
(Figure~\ref{fig:budget}). There is a narrow band, $\dw\!\in\![0.31, 0.62]$, in which
adaptation is near its maximum while retention stays near the base ceiling. Adapters that
fall off the frontier do so because their (init~$\times$~learning-rate) combination pushes
$\dw$ past the right edge of this band. Weight decay penalizes $\lVert A\rVert^2 +
\lVert B\rVert^2$ regardless of subspace, so it \emph{bounds} $\dw$ and lands the model
inside the band---LoRA{+}wd sits at $0.394$, the un-regularized high-adapters at or past
the edge. That is the entire mechanism of Claim 2: the geometric adapters are elaborate
ways to choose \emph{where} the update goes; weight decay is the trivial way to choose
\emph{how big} it is, and how big is what matters.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
% ===========================================================================
We state every limitation explicitly; these are the reviewer attack surface, and owning
them is the defense.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single seed (seed 42).} This is fine for the \emph{law}---49 points trace
        the curve robustly---but \emph{not} for any head-to-head \emph{ranking} claim.
        A prior three-seed matrix exposed single-seed ``collapse basins'' (a change of
        seed swung some cells by tens of points). The LoRA{+}wd Pareto margin
        ($+1.5$\,pp adaptation, $+0.8$\,pp retention over the runners-up) is smaller than
        that documented seed variance, so it is presented as ``matches/edges,'' and error
        bars (seeds 43/44) on the $\sim$6--8 headline cells are required before any
        per-method delta is headlined.
        %TODO(experiment): B5c seeds 43/44 on the ~6-8 headline cells not yet collected.
  \item \textbf{Rank and knob asymmetry.} Ranks are not matched (LoRA/DoRA/CorDA/LoRA-Null
        r16; MiLoRA/SC-LoRA r32; CLoRA $k{=}1024$), and \emph{only} LoRA received the
        weight-decay knob. A referee may fairly say LoRA{+}wd wins because it has more
        capacity and an extra regularizer no other method got. The \emph{law} framing
        sidesteps this (it is a statement about $\dw$, not a ranking), but the strong form
        of Claim 2 needs a param-matched LoRA{+}wd control (r16 and r32) and, ideally, the
        weight-decay knob given to every frontier arm.

        %wd-on-every-frontier-arm not yet run.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================
The field is optimizing the wrong variable. Across the six of eight adapters we can
currently assess fairly (SC-LoRA the one provisional deviator, CorDA
withheld---Section~\ref{sec:limits}), two domains, and a partial second model,
catastrophic forgetting under PEFT is governed by a single scalar,
the magnitude of the weight update $\dw$, and geometry---the choice of \emph{where} the
update goes---is close to causally inert once that magnitude is controlled. The practical
consequence is deflationary: plain LoRA with a weight-decay term matches or edges the
adaptation--retention frontier of every elaborate geometric adapter we tested, because
weight decay is the most direct way to land inside the magnitude sweet spot. The
diagnosis exhibits the \emph{ingredients} of a learning-rate artifact behind the
geometric adapters' reported wins---each peaks at a different rate, so a single
fixed-rate comparison flatters whichever method is well tuned there, and the $\dw$ an
adapter produces predicts retention far better than its learning rate does, so a
per-method sweep collapses the difference back onto the shared curve. Reproducing a
specific published win and then dissolving it under the sweep is deferred
(Section~\ref{sec:lr}).

We have been careful about the strength of each claim. The law (Claim 1) is mature and
robust on Llama-2. The consequence and diagnosis (Claims 2 and 3) are supported by the
present Llama-2 evidence at the honest strength of ``matches/edges'' and
``best-LR-is-not-shared,'' and become fully airtight only once the deferred controls land:
seeds on the headline cells, a param-matched LoRA{+}wd control with weight decay applied
to every frontier arm, an eval-matched calibration for the data-aware inits, CorDA
re-instated (or openly declared off-curve), and the completed Qwen 2$\times$2. Should
those controls change the rankings, the paper still stands as a \emph{measurement}
contribution: the learning-rate-sweep-per-method is an instrument that decomposes any
adapter's reported win into its true magnitude effect plus a learning-rate-tuning
artifact, and the token-weighted Frobenius axis is the fair way to compare adapters.

The message for a field that ships a new adapter every week is a wake-up call:
\textbf{control the magnitude, not the geometry.}

% ===========================================================================
\section*{Future Work}
% ===========================================================================
Beyond the controls in Section~\ref{sec:limits}, three directions follow naturally.
\emph{CorDA++} (dynamic covariance selection and dynamic rank allocation under a fixed
parameter budget) is the strongest 2025-era geometric method and the right target to land
the negative result against, at a realized-parameter-count matched budget. \emph{Eval-matched
calibration arms} would settle whether the data-aware inits' residual penalty is geometry
or a calibration artifact---if they return to the curve, the cleaner result is ``the law
is method-free once calibration is fair.'' Finally, a \emph{gated-magnitude adapter}---an
input-conditional gate scaling only the adapter delta, trained against a preservation
corpus disjoint from the evaluation set---is the constructive follow-up, now with the
higher bar of having to beat LoRA{+}wd rather than the elaborate adapters.
