\begin{abstract}
% ===========================================================================
A rich family of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapters spanning
data-aware initializations, null-space projections, orthogonality penalties, and
magnitude--direction decompositions aims to preserve a pretrained model's
general capabilities while adapting it to a new task. The literature already
carries early signals of what governs this retention: update-magnitude and
cross-entropy-drift diagnostics on one hand, and the geometry of initializations
and subspaces on the other. We bring these threads together in a systematic
evaluation of what affects adaptation and retention: Every adapter is measured
identically under \emph{four instruments}: the adaptation-retention trade-off,
a method-neutral update-magnitude axis ($\dw$), update geometry of the major and minor components in the SVD spectrum, and behavioral
(cross-entropy) drift, swept over learning rates and seeds across two
architectures and two task types (1{,}035 adapters). Retention tracks update
magnitude to first order (pooled $r\!=\!-0.847$, flat-then-falling with a
per-family knee); once magnitude is fixed, update-shape geometry adds
$\approx\!23\times$ less in a nested variance decomposition, and per-method
differences carry a learning-rate-tuning component that a sweep absorbs. Plain
LoRA{+}weight-decay, the cheapest magnitude control, matches or edges the
adaptation-retention frontier of the geometric adapters, at far lower cost.
Across every family, recipe, and adapter we measure, it is the size of the
update, not its geometry, that tracks forgetting.
\end{abstract}

% ===========================================================================
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================
Catastrophic forgetting---the erosion of a pretrained model's general
capabilities when it is fine-tuned on a narrow task---is a central obstacle to
deploying adapted large language models. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT),
and in particular LoRA~\citep{hu2021lora} and its descendants, was supposed to
help: restricting the update to a low-rank correction $\Delta W = BA$ should leave
the bulk of the pretrained weights untouched. Residual forgetting persists, and a
family of \emph{geometric} adapters addresses it by choosing \emph{where} the
update goes: from the principal or minor singular directions of the pretrained
weight (PiSSA~\citep{meng2024pissa}, MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora}); by projecting
orthogonally to the dominant weight subspace (OPLoRA~\citep{xiong2025oplora}); by a
data- or activation-covariance decomposition that freezes a ``knowledge'' subspace
(CorDA~\citep{yang2024corda}, SC-LoRA~\citep{luo2025sclora},
LoRA-Null~\citep{tang2025loranull}); by an orthogonality penalty against a frozen
subspace (CLoRA~\citep{clora}); or by decomposing magnitude from direction
(DoRA~\citep{liu2024dora}). Alongside these designs, the literature carries
early signals of what governs retention itself: CLoRA reports an effective
update-magnitude diagnostic and a cross-entropy-drift view of forgetting, and
the initialization- and subspace-based methods embody geometric hypotheses.
These signals have so far been studied one method and one axis at a time.

\paragraph{Contributions.} This work systematically evaluates what affects
adaptation and retention across these adapters. Every adapter is measured
identically under four instruments---(i) the adaptation--retention trade-off,
(ii) a method-neutral update-magnitude axis $\dw$ (CLoRA's $\mathbb{F}$
diagnostic, Section~\ref{sec:instruments}), (iii) update geometry, and (iv)
behavioral cross-entropy (CE) drift---each swept over per-method learning rates
and, on the Llama spine, seeds, with eval-matched calibration for the data-aware
initializations. The evaluation yields a finding: forgetting is governed,
\emph{to first order}, by the magnitude of the update, essentially independently
of adapter geometry. Magnitude is the \emph{first-order predictor and control
variable}, not the proximal mechanism---our mediation analysis puts behavioral
drift downstream of magnitude and upstream of benchmark loss
(Section~\ref{sec:drift}).

\paragraph{The spine: three observations.}
\textbf{(1) The magnitude relation.} Across 1{,}035 adapters spanning four
model$\times$task settings under six training recipes and two architectures,
retention tracks $\log\dw$ at pooled $r\!=\!-0.847$ (rank $-0.923$),
flat-then-falling with a per-family knee (Section~\ref{sec:relation}); an
interventional arm upgrades this from correlation toward control.
\textbf{(2) Geometry adds little once magnitude is controlled.} With $\dw$ fixed,
update-shape geometry adds only $\Delta R^2\!=\!+0.017$ ($\approx\!23\times$ less than magnitude's
$+0.395$) and method identity $+0.006$ (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}).
\textbf{(3) Learning rate is only a proxy.} Different adapters transmit the same
learning rate into different $\dw$, so the reported per-method wins carry the
\emph{ingredients} of a learning-rate artifact that a per-method sweep dissolves
(Section~\ref{sec:lr}); the practical consequence is that plain
LoRA{+}weight-decay---the simplest magnitude control---matches or edges the
frontier of every geometric adapter at far lower engineering cost
(Section~\ref{sec:corollary}). \textbf{In sum:} across the audit it is the size
of the update, not its geometry, that tracks forgetting; geometry mostly sets
\emph{where} on the magnitude curve a method starts.

\paragraph{Delta over prior work, and honest coverage.} The measurement is what
separates the finding from prior single-method or adaptation-only studies. CLoRA
argues from one method; the concurrent adaptation study of \citet{lee2026lr}
shows peak \emph{adaptation} converges once each variant is tuned at its own
learning rate, but does not study retention. We put competing geometries on a
controlled-magnitude axis and add an interventional test (§\ref{sec:relation})
that no correlational comparison can offer. We state coverage plainly: of the
\emph{eight} adapters in the study design we assess \emph{seven} (CorDA is
withheld pending a calibration-fairness fix), plus three control arms (full
fine-tuning, a rescaling intervention, and off-recipe bridging tasks). Across the
seven, retention lies on a single $\dw$ curve; SC-LoRA's lone
apparent below-curve deviation resolves, under an eval-matched calibration
control, into a calibration artifact rather than a geometric penalty
(Section~\ref{sec:corollary}). We scope the causal claim to Llama-2 commonsense,
where the interventional arm ran, and the geometry verdict to the LoRA-variant
family, where all seven adapters live.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}\label{sec:related}
% ===========================================================================
\paragraph{The adapter family, organized by basis.} The forgetting-mitigation
literature is best organized not by surface mechanism but by the single
load-bearing axis on which the methods differ: the \emph{basis} in which they
reason about which directions to protect.
\emph{(i) Static weight-SVD basis}: initialize from the top or minor singular
triples of $W_0$ (PiSSA~\citep{meng2024pissa}, MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora}), or
project into the orthogonal complement of the top-$k$ subspace
(OPLoRA~\citep{xiong2025oplora}). \emph{(ii) Data/activation-covariance basis}:
decompose $W_0$ against a calibration covariance and freeze the components it
exercises (CorDA~\citep{yang2024corda}, SC-LoRA~\citep{luo2025sclora},
LoRA-Null~\citep{tang2025loranull}; CorDA++~\citep{yang2025cordapp} adds dynamic
selection). \emph{(iii) Auxiliary loss / other}: penalize adapter overlap with a
fixed subspace (CLoRA~\citep{clora}), or decompose magnitude from direction
(DoRA~\citep{liu2024dora}). What every one shares is a commitment to
\emph{geometry}: the premise that \emph{where} the update lands governs
forgetting. Our audit finds that retention instead tracks \emph{how big} the
update is.

\paragraph{Magnitude and forgetting, before and outside this family.} That
forgetting tracks how far the weights move is not new; what is new is showing it is the
\emph{first-order} story once the measurement is fair. EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}
and L$^2$-SP~\citep{li2018l2sp} penalize deviation from the pretrained weights
(with and without a geometric weighting); \citet{kalajdzievski2024scaling} fit
scaling laws in which forgetting grows with parameters tuned and steps taken
(proxies for update size); and \citet{biderman2024lora} show LoRA forgets less
than full fine-tuning while learning less. Even within this family, MiLoRA's
WikiText forgetting loss orders PiSSA${>}$LoRA${>}$MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora} and
LoRA-Null's Table~4b shows CorDA's retention falling as its
rank~grows~\citep{tang2025loranull}. Each is reported for one method's own design;
none measures $\dw$ across methods, compares geometries on a common magnitude
axis, or sweeps the learning rate per method---so none can ask whether geometry
adds anything \emph{beyond} magnitude, our question.

\paragraph{Reconciling an apparent counterexample.} \citet{biderman2024lora}
tested weight decay for \emph{full} fine-tuning and found it learned and forgot
like the unregularized baseline---seemingly against our Claim~3. The results are
consistent under the magnitude account: they applied tiny coefficients
($5\times10^{-5}$, $10^{-4}$) to all seven billion parameters at a single tuned
rate, barely moving $\dw$; we apply weight decay $0.3$ to the adapter matrices
alone under a per-method sweep, which demonstrably walks the model down the
retention curve (Section~\ref{sec:corollary}). Their single-rate design is itself
an instance of the confound our Claim~3 is about. CLoRA's published numbers are
faithful and recur as external corroboration throughout
(Appendix~\ref{app:clora}); our research question is only whether LoRA{+}wd
matches them at matched capacity.

\section{The Four Instruments}\label{sec:instruments}
% ===========================================================================
A fair cross-method comparison of \emph{forgetting} cannot be assembled from
published numbers, because the adapter papers do not measure retention on a common
axis: only CLoRA~\citep{clora} reports our benchmarks (BBH, MMLU-Pro); the
data-aware inits report closed-book factoid QA; MiLoRA a WikiText loss; PiSSA and
DoRA no forgetting metric at all. Evaluating every adapter ourselves under one
harness is therefore a precondition, not a convenience---and it is the
measurement contribution. We run four measurement instruments and two comparison
instruments identically on every adapter.

\paragraph{Setup.} Our primary, complete evidence is \textbf{Llama-2-7B}; a
replication on \textbf{Qwen2.5-7B} covers commonsense in full and math
qualitatively (Section~\ref{sec:relation}). We fine-tune on \emph{commonsense}
(the eight-task LLM-Adapters suite~\citep{hu2023llmadapters}) and \emph{math}
(MetaMathQA, evaluated on GSM8K), giving \textbf{four model$\times$task settings
under six training recipes and two architectures}. Every structured adapter is
implemented as ``LoRA with a different initialization'' loaded into one shared
PEFT trainer, so the only difference between arms is the init routine (and, for
CLoRA, one added loss term). We study \textbf{eight adapters}: LoRA (r16),
LoRA{+}wd (r32, decay $0.3$ on the adapter matrices), DoRA (r16), CorDA (r16),
MiLoRA (r32), SC-LoRA (r32), LoRA-Null (r16), CLoRA ($k{=}1024$). PiSSA is run at
its recipe learning rate (it has a math operating point and a fingerprint) but not
given the full seven-rate sweep; OPLoRA is positioned but not run. We sweep seven
learning rates
$\{2,5\}{\times}10^{-5}$, $\{1,2,3,5\}{\times}10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ per method; two
higher rates diverged to NaN on every method and are dropped. Our plain-LoRA CS-8
($79.1$ at the canonical $3{\times}10^{-4}$) sits between the published Llama-2-7B
LoRA baselines ($77.6$/$79.9$~\citep{clora}), so the scale is trustworthy in
absolute terms. \emph{Retention (core)} is mean(BBH answer-only 3-shot, MMLU-Pro
5-shot CoT); its no-fine-tuning ceiling is $\mathbf{26.0}$. \emph{Adaptation} is
CS-8 or GSM8K exact-match.

\paragraph{Instrument (ii): the magnitude axis $\dw$.} Our governing variable is
the \textbf{effective update magnitude}: the mean normalized output displacement
the update induces on real token traffic, exactly CLoRA's $\mathbb{F}$ forgetting
diagnostic~\citep{clora} (their Eq.~3). With $\mathcal{L}$ the adapted modules,
$\Delta W_\ell$ module $\ell$'s total update (initialization residual included,
so a $0$-step run gives $\Delta W = 0$), and $x_{\ell,t}$ token $t$'s input
activation captured by forward hooks over $100$ held-out evaluation prompts (the
same probe set for every run),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fdelta}
  \dw \;=\; \frac{1}{\sum_{\ell} N_\ell}
  \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}} \sum_{t=1}^{N_\ell}
  \frac{\lVert \Delta W_\ell\, x_{\ell,t} \rVert_2}{\lVert x_{\ell,t} \rVert_2}.
\end{equation}
Our $\dw$ is on the same normalized scale as CLoRA's Table~4
(Appendix~\ref{app:clora}). We adopt it over the spectral norm $\smax$, which
spiky inits (CorDA, DoRA, SC-LoRA) inflate: among candidate axes $\dw$ gives the
tightest, most method-neutral retention fit ($R^2\!=\!0.72$ vs.\ $0.56$ for
$\lVert\Delta W\rVert_F$ and $0.58$ for $\smax$; Figure~\ref{fig:axis}), so it is
the fair $x$-axis throughout.

  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_magnitude_law.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Instrument (ii): choosing a fair magnitude axis.} Retention
  versus three candidate magnitude measures (log $x$-axis), pooled over adapters.

\paragraph{Instruments (iii)--(iv), briefly.} \emph{Update geometry} projects each
trained $\Delta W=(\alpha/r)BA$ onto the base weight's singular subspaces
($e_{\rm top}/e_{\rm bot}$, stable rank), computed from the saved adapters without
retraining. \emph{Behavioral drift} is the cross-entropy (equivalently KL) of the
fine-tuned model to the base on held-out text, the proxy MiLoRA reports following
\citet{kalajdzievski2024scaling}.

\paragraph{Comparison instruments: the per-method LR sweep and eval-matched
calibration.} Two design choices make the comparison fair. First, \emph{every}
method receives the same seven-rate sweep and is reported at its own best-adapt
rate---the control this literature omits, since MiLoRA, CLoRA, LoRA-Null, and
CorDA++ each fix one shared rate and none logs the $\dw$ it produces
(Section~\ref{sec:lr}). Second, because the data-aware inits calibrate their
covariance on factoid QA (nq\_open) while retention is measured on reasoning
benchmarks, we add an \emph{eval-matched} calibration control that recalibrates on
evaluation-representative text---the control that turns SC-LoRA's apparent
geometric penalty into a calibration artifact (Section~\ref{sec:corollary}).

\paragraph{The frozen pool.} All numbers live on one frozen pool of
\textbf{1{,}035 adapters} (finite $\dw\!>\!0$ and finite retention;
CorDA/CorDA++ withheld) spanning the six recipe families over seeds 42--46; the
per-family counts and a full reconciliation table (date, filter, join, $n$) are in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\section{The Magnitude Relation}\label{sec:relation}
% ===========================================================================
Our central result is an empirical regularity: retention is a tight, monotone,
\emph{method-independent} function of $\dw$. Figure~\ref{fig:hero} is the hero
exhibit; plotting core retention against $\dw$, adapters interleave along one
downward curve.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\columnwidth]{fig0_hero.png}
  \caption{\textbf{The magnitude relation.} Core retention (mean of BBH and
  MMLU-Pro) versus $\dw$ (log scale), Llama-2-7B commonsense. Adapters interleave
  along one curve; method identity is not a second axis. Green dotted line: base
  ceiling ($26.0$). The pooled relation holds across all $1{,}035$ adapters
  (text); this panel is the Llama-2 commonsense view. The star marks the
  LoRA{+}wd operating point (Table~\ref{tab:cs}): the cheapest magnitude control
  sits at the low-$\dw$, high-retention end of the same curve.}
  \label{fig:hero}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The relation, at scale.} Pooled over all $1{,}035$ adapters, retention
correlates with $\log_{10}\dw$ at $r\!=\!-0.847$ (Spearman $-0.923$), and the
per-family correlations run $-0.830$ to $-0.929$---both models, both task types,
six recipes. Seed-averaged cell-level correlations agree ($-0.799$ to $-0.928$),
and within-cell seed noise is small (retention SD $0.3$--$1.0$\,pp on Llama,
$2.1$--$2.7$\,pp on Qwen) against a $15$--$30$\,pp retention range. The functional
form is \emph{flat-then-falling}: a two-segment fit beats a line in every family,
with per-family knees in $\log_{10}\dw$ from $-0.91$ to $-0.02$; below the knee
the slope is flat-to-mild, above it steep ($-7.5$ to $-40.8$\,pp/decade). Because
the normalized slopes do not converge ($-0.33$ to $-0.70$), we call this a
\emph{magnitude relation with a knee}, reserving ``law'' for the knee-qualified
statement.

\paragraph{The trade-off exists only at the frontier.} Pooled, adaptation and
retention are \emph{positively} associated (over-dosed runs lose both; the sign
attenuates but persists when quarantined far-collapse runs are excluded), so ``you
must trade retention for adaptation'' holds only along the dose axis \emph{within}
a recipe. Operating-point comparisons (Section~\ref{sec:corollary}), not pooled
scatter, are the right cross-method tool.

\paragraph{From correlation toward control.} The raw correlation ``a bigger
update forgets more'' is partly true by construction, so we do not rest the claim
on it. Two stronger tests. \emph{(i) Within-cell micro-test.} At a fixed recipe,
seed-level $\log\dw$ fluctuations predict seed-level retention fluctuations at
demeaned $r\!=\!-0.713$ ($n\!=\!954$ observations over $290$ cells)---the effect
survives after every recipe knob is held constant. \emph{(ii) Intervention
(E1, Llama-CS).} Rescaling a \emph{trained} adapter's update to a new magnitude
(no retraining) lands it on the curve: $15/15$ rescales fall on-curve
(mean residual $+1.29\!\pm\!2.07$\,pp), while $9/9$ random-direction updates at
matched magnitude land $\approx\!1.8$\,pp below the curve
($-1.76\!\pm\!1.32$; a $-3.05$\,pp gap versus the trained rescales at matched
magnitude) \emph{and} buy essentially zero adaptation. Direction adapts; magnitude
forgets. We scope this causal reading to Llama-2 commonsense, where the
intervention ran (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Second-architecture and second-domain replication.} The relation
reproduces on Qwen2.5-7B commonsense ($r\!=\!-0.840$, $n\!=\!151$; the pooled $r$
is indistinguishable from Llama's, the steeper slope reflecting Qwen's uncalibrated
retention scale, so we never merge the two into one fit), on Qwen math (three
seeds, $n\!=\!164$, $r\!=\!-0.830$; BBH-only, Qwen ceilings open), on Llama math
($r\!=\!-0.865$ sweep, $-0.929$ grid), and off-recipe on a third task (E7, MedMCQA:
$-0.878$ Llama, $-0.995$ Qwen). A full fine-tuning anchor (E2) is monotone in the
same form but sits $4$--$9$\,pp \emph{below} the LoRA-family curve at matched $\dw$
(dense $\Delta W$ under-counts on our probe): universal in \emph{form},
family-specific in \emph{level}.

\paragraph{One ordering, per capability.} Normalizing each retention benchmark's
degradation slope by its base ceiling, the fragility ordering is identical in all
six families---MMLU-Pro ${>}$ BBH ${>}$ MMLU ${>}$ TruthfulQA (Kendall
$W\!=\!1.000$; distance-to-chance-floor caveat noted)---%
format-following capability dies first, calibration-style knowledge last. That a
single scalar induces a stable, falsifiable per-capability ordering is evidence
the phenomenon is not a single-metric artifact (a TruthfulQA sign-flip caveat is
in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Geometry Adds Little Once Magnitude Is Controlled}\label{sec:geometry}
% ===========================================================================
The load-bearing negative result is that adapter \emph{geometry}---the subspace an
initialization or penalty steers the update into---adds little to retention once
$\dw$ is fixed. This is the statement no single-method comparison
(CLoRA) or adaptation-only study (\citealp{lee2026lr}) is positioned to make, and
we make it with one exhibit: a nested variance decomposition on all $1{,}034$
adapters (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).

\input{tables/table_ladder.tex}

\paragraph{The ladder.} Starting from family fixed effects ($R^2\!=\!0.390$),
adding $\log\dw$ raises $R^2$ to $0.785$ ($\Delta R^2\!=\!+0.395$); adding update
geometry ($e_{\rm top}$, stable rank) raises it to $0.802$ ($+0.017$); adding
method identity to $0.808$ ($+0.006$). Magnitude's incremental $\Delta R^2$ is
$\approx\!23\times$ geometry's ($0.395$ vs.\ $0.017$). An exogenous-blocks
commonality split (magnitude vs.\ shape geometry, the causal-knob comparison)
gives unique(magnitude) $+0.296$, unique(geometry) $+0.016$, shared $+0.099$---an
$\approx\!18\times$ ratio in \emph{unique} variance; the standardized
coefficient of $\log\dw$ is $-0.744$ $[-0.894,-0.615]$ against geometry
$|\beta|\!\le\!0.138$. Cell-level cluster bootstraps ($B\!=\!2000$) put the
magnitude${>}$geometry ordering in $2000/2000$ replicates, and the
cluster-robust $t(\log\dw)$ is $-7.9$ (decisive). We quote $\Delta R^2$
magnitudes and these CIs rather than any naive $F$; one candidate direction term
(top-singular alignment) is significant only before clustering and dies after it,
so we drop it and use $e_{\rm top}$/stable rank as the geometry block.

\paragraph{Geometry is identity, not retention.} The same measurements that rule
geometry out as a \emph{lever} make it an excellent \emph{fingerprint}: the
update's stable rank separates methods cleanly at matched $\dw$ (DoRA $4.5$, LoRA
$5.0$, the mid-cluster $8$--$9$, SC-LoRA $14.5$, PiSSA $18.1$; run-level,
descriptive) and recovers each method's advertised design from its trained weights
(MiLoRA minor, SC-LoRA input-principal, CLoRA MLP), recurring even at $284$B scale
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). But its retention leverage after controlling
$\dw$ is bounded and \emph{model-dependent}: partial $r(\text{stable
rank},\text{ret}\mid\log\dw)$ is $-0.3$ to $-0.67$ on the four Llama families yet
$\approx\!0$ on Qwen. Geometry sets \emph{where on the magnitude axis a method
starts}---a principal-space init begins aligned with the loss gradient and
accumulates a larger update---not a dimension orthogonal to it. We scope this
verdict to the LoRA-variant family (all seven assessed adapters); data-aware inits
are covered via the calibration control of Section~\ref{sec:corollary}, and CorDA
is withheld. The fingerprint panel and per-family partials are in
Appendix~\ref{app:figs} (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}).

% ===========================================================================
\section{Learning Rate Is Only a Proxy}\label{sec:lr}
% ===========================================================================
If forgetting is governed by $\dw$, learning rate should matter only insofar as it
sets $\dw$---and, critically, different adapters should transmit the \emph{same}
rate into \emph{different} $\dw$. Both hold, and together they are the mechanism
of the reported ``wins.''

\paragraph{$\dw$ predicts retention better than the learning rate.} $\dw$ beats
the learning rate in every family ($R^2(\dw)\!=\!0.69$--$0.86$ vs.\
$0.22$--$0.52$), and not as a coarse-grid artifact: holding LR constant, $\dw$
still predicts retention (fixed-LR strata $r\!\le\!-0.7$ at every rate
$\ge\!10^{-4}$), and partialling leaves $\dw$'s signal intact while LR's nearly
vanishes ($r(\dw,\text{ret}\mid\text{LR})\!=\!-0.58$ to $-0.91$ vs.\
$r(\text{LR},\text{ret}\mid\dw)\!\le\!+0.29$). In the metric league table
(Appendix~\ref{app:figs}, Table~\ref{tab:league}), LR ranks fifth of the ten
metrics we rank ($\Delta R^2\!=\!+0.207$), below every magnitude measure and
behavioral drift, above every geometry metric.

\paragraph{The best rate is not shared, and neither is $\dw$-per-LR.} Each method
peaks at a different rate (SC-LoRA turns a given rate into the largest $\dw$
everywhere; LoRA{+}wd and CLoRA the smallest), so a single fixed rate flatters
whichever method happens to be well-tuned there---precisely how single-LR
comparisons manufacture wins. Figure~\ref{fig:lrartifact} makes the illusion
explicit: for each structured adapter we take its best-looking single rate and
compare against plain LoRA at that same rate; several look far better
(SC-LoRA $+26$\,pp of adaptation, LoRA-Null $+19.5$). But once the rate is swept,
the LoRA{+}wd frontier sits on or above every one of those points on both axes.
The apparent win decomposes into a learning-rate-tuning effect that vanishes under
the sweep plus a residual that lands back on the shared curve.

\section{Behavioral Drift: The Same Axis, Read in Behavior Space}\label{sec:drift}
% ===========================================================================
The fourth instrument, CE-to-base drift, deepens the finding and yields a
practical corollary. It is a \emph{behavior-space view of the same drift}, not
independent confirmation: $r(\text{KL},\text{retention})\!=\!-0.63$ to $-0.92$ per
family, but in the nested ladder KL explains $+0.340$ of retention variance alone
(vs.\ magnitude's $+0.420$) and only $+0.005$ \emph{beyond} magnitude---drift is
downstream. Our mediation places KL as the \emph{proximal} channel in four of six
families with magnitude the upstream control, which is why we call magnitude the
first-order predictor, not the mechanism. Magnitude keeps $+0.085$ beyond CE: a
second, format-following channel (MMLU-Pro answer format) that neutral-text CE
cannot see.

\paragraph{A regularity a practitioner could exploit.} KL needs only one forward
pass over held-out text---no benchmark harness, no adapter weights. We observe
that in four of six families, across both base models, the retention knee falls at
$\approx\!0.26$--$0.30$ nats of KL drift, and that a per-family knee-calibrated
KL$\rightarrow$retention mapping predicts held-out retention at RMSE
$1.3$--$2.0$\,pp on the four Llama families ($\approx\!2\times$ the retention
eval's own seed noise). As a \emph{damage tripwire} $\log\dw$ is equally good (KL
AUC $\ge\!0.976$ in all six families, $\log\dw$ $0.98$--$1.00$); KL's distinction
is quantitative calibration and needing no adapter access, not better screening. On
Qwen it is a tripwire, not a ruler (RMSE $\approx\!4$--$6$\,pp, tail-driven). See
Appendix~\ref{app:figs}, Figure~\ref{fig:ceproxy}.

\section{The Practical Corollary and the Cost of Geometry}\label{sec:corollary}
% ===========================================================================
\input{tables/table_cost_of_geometry.tex}
If retention tracks $\dw$, then the simplest way to bound it---a weight-decay term
on the adapter matrices, no geometric construction---would land on the frontier of
the geometric adapters. It does, and the way it does is the point: \textbf{LoRA{+}wd
reaches the frontier by moving \emph{along} the shared curve to a smaller
magnitude, not by beating the curve.}

\paragraph{On the frontier, multi-seed, four families.} Table~\ref{tab:cs} reports
each method at its best-adapt learning rate on Llama commonsense (multi-seed).
LoRA{+}wd(0.3) reaches CS-8 $81.8\!\pm\!0.2$ at core retention $25.9\!\pm\!0.4$
(within $0.1$\,pp of the base ceiling) at the smallest update in the table---the
highest-adaptation and highest-retention point, with the tightest seed noise and
widest safe band. In the multi-seed Pareto audit (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}) it is
the sole method on the observed (deterministic) frontier in three of four families
(the one-cell E6 MiLoRA{+}wd arm aside), losing none of $26$ paired head-to-head
comparisons on retention. A parameter-matched control closes the capacity reading:
LoRA{+}wd at rank~16 reproduces the operating point at plain LoRA's parameter count
(retention $26.27$ at $\dw\!=\!0.334$), while a plain-LoRA rank ladder at fixed
$3{\times}10^{-4}$ slides \emph{down} the shared curve as $\dw$ grows---rank buys
magnitude, not retention.

\paragraph{In-harness, both domains.} On the faithful published math recipe
(rank~64, CLoRA at $k{=}256$ per the source papers---a protocol change from the CS
study's r16/r32/$k$1024, not an inconsistency), LoRA{+}wd(0.3) reaches GSM8K
$66.8\!\pm\!0.8$ at BBH $33.6\!\pm\!1.0$ (three seeds; retention at or above the
$33.1$ base), edging our in-harness CLoRA-$k$256 reproduction ($60.8$) by
$+6.0$\,pp GSM8K at a quarter of its update magnitude. We anchor the CLoRA
comparison \emph{in-harness} (both swept) and use ``matches or edges'';
cross-harness deltas are not claimed (full table, Table~\ref{tab:math}).

\paragraph{The cost of geometry.} The ``for free'' is literal: weight decay is a
single AdamW flag with no init pass, while every geometric adapter pays strictly
more for the same budget (train time, resident memory, or an initialization tax)
for a retention gap at matched adaptation that is negative in every family
(Table~\ref{tab:cost}).

\paragraph{The one reversal, scoped honestly.} There is one within-harness
head-to-head loss: SC-LoRA on Qwen-math reaches GSM8K $77.2\!\pm\!0.8$ at
statistically tied BBH, $+8.3$\,pp over LoRA{+}wd (paired over three seeds,
$t\!=\!5.2$; on the observed frontier). We read this as a genuine win for
SC-LoRA's \emph{data-aware initialization as configured} (with nq\_open
calibration), not for subspace geometry per se: the win occurs at a below-knee,
low-magnitude cell ($\dw\!=\!0.107$, the second-smallest of its own sweep and
essentially LoRA{+}wd's operating-point magnitude)---the counterexample wins at
small magnitude, not by tolerating a large one. And the scope is narrow: our E4
control (Llama-CS, retention-side) showed SC-LoRA's outcomes shift $\sim\!4$\,pp
with the calibration corpus, no such control exists for this Qwen-math family, and
the win buys the study's worst seed instability (Qwen-CS adaptation a seed lottery,
$\pm\!16$\,pp) and narrowest safe band.

\paragraph{A free-lunch region below the knee.} Using the frozen per-family knees,
$99$--$100\%$ of each family's peak achievable adaptation lies \emph{below} the
retention knee---no achievable adaptation requires paying measurable retention. We
also observe that reaching that region tracks the presence of a magnitude knob: at
the standard $3{\times}10^{-4}$, plain LoRA has \emph{zero} grid cells below the
knee, while $\approx\!40$--$46\%$ of LoRA{+}wd grid cells ($12$ of $26$) sit there
at $81.4$ adaptation. Indeed weight decay, CLoRA's null-space dimension $k$, and LoRA rank
each move $\dw$ monotonically and each has its retention effect vanish once $\dw$
is known (residual $\Delta R^2\!\le\!0.006$), landing on one curve
(Figure~\ref{fig:dose}). That raising $k$ at fixed LR trades adaptation for
magnitude localizes the $k$-knob's retention effect to dose reduction; it does not
dispute CLoRA's published high-$k$ wins, which sit at different operating points
and are faithful.

\paragraph{Where weight decay stops being free.} Weight decay is not universally a
free knob: it \emph{transfers} to MiLoRA (MiLoRA{+}wd lands $+1.75$/$+2.36$\,pp
above the curve at CS-8 $80.2$) but \emph{breaks} DoRA as implemented (naive AdamW
decay on DoRA's magnitude vector is degenerate)---a boundary we report rather than
claim wd as a drop-in for every adapter.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================
The field has been optimizing a second-order variable. Forgetting under PEFT is
governed, to first order, by the update magnitude $\dw$; update-shape geometry
adds $\approx\!23\times$ less (nested; $\approx\!18\times$ less unique variance)
and no retention at matched adaptation, behavioral drift is the same axis read
in behavior space, and learning rate is only a proxy for it. The consequence is
deflationary: plain LoRA{+}weight-decay matches or edges the frontier of every
geometric adapter at far lower cost. Even if outstanding controls shift a
ranking, the measurement contribution stands: the four instruments decompose any
reported win into magnitude, learning-rate-tuning, and calibration components.
Across every family, recipe, and adapter we measured, the size of the update---not
its geometry---is the variable that tracks forgetting.

\section*{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% ===========================================================================
Table~\ref{tab:claims} maps each claim to the strength of its evidence and the
experiment that closes its gap; the detail follows.

\input{tables/table_claims_evidence.tex}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The causal test is single-arm.} The interventional evidence (E1)
        ran on Llama-2 commonsense only ($n\!=\!24$: $15$ rescales, $9$
        random-direction controls). We scope ``magnitude controls forgetting'' as
        a causal statement to that setting; everywhere else the evidence is
        correlational (albeit multi-seed, within-cell, and cross-architecture). A
        Qwen rescaling arm is the highest-value missing experiment.
  \item \textbf{The geometry verdict is scoped to the LoRA-variant family.} All
        seven assessed adapters are LoRA variants; the $\Delta R^2\!=\!+0.017$
        geometry ceiling is established there. Data-aware inits are covered only
        via the eval-matched calibration control (SC-LoRA: nq\_open $-3.39$\,pp
        $\to$ eval-matched $+0.92$\,pp above curve, $n\!=\!20/24$; a favorable
        control, held-out-representative calibration is the fair target), and the
        second-order geometry effect is itself model-dependent (present on Llama,
        $\approx\!0$ on Qwen).
  \item \textbf{CorDA is withheld from every quantitative claim.} CorDA was
        mis-calibrated (wikitext-2 rather than nq\_open) and its $\dw$ explodes on
        the current port; it enters no law fit, table, or ladder, and appears only
        as a qualitative fingerprint. The study therefore assesses seven of its
        eight adapters, not all eight---a real gap, since CorDA is among the
        adapters most likely to stress the geometry-null.
        %TODO(experiment): CorDA nq_open re-run + 0-step self-check + fair calibration before re-entry.
  \item \textbf{Plain-LoRA robustness is $5/7$, and the LoRA-Null labeling bug.}
        The data generator classified the distinct LoRA-Null adapter as
        ``lora'', so a pooled ``LoRA'' series reports robustness $10/14$; the
        plain-LoRA subgroup alone is $5/7$. We report $5/7$ and split LoRA-Null as
        its own series throughout; the legend fix is deferred to camera-ready.
  \item \textbf{DoRA's $\dw$ is a lower bound (at submission).} We compute $\dw$
        from PEFT's \texttt{get\_delta\_weight}, which for DoRA omits the
        forward-time magnitude-vector rescaling; DoRA's \emph{retention} is
        measured correctly, but its $\dw$ coordinate is under-counted, so DoRA may
        sit slightly right of where we plot it. The correction can only increase
        its already-positive residual, not pull it below the curve. A corrected
        recompute is deferred to camera-ready.
        %TODO(experiment): recompute DoRA fdelta with the magnitude-vector rescaling (needs checkpoints + GPU).
  \item \textbf{Scale: 7B only; 284B designed-but-lost.} A DeepSeek-V4 284B
        generalization run trained but its retention evaluations never synced
        before the fleet was killed; we make \emph{no} 284B
        retention/CE/magnitude-relation claim. Post-freeze salvage shows only that
        the update-\emph{shape} fingerprint recurs (the residual-vs-direct design
        dichotomy; directional consistency, $6/6$ families positive, sign test
        $p\!\approx\!0.03$), and that recurrence carries a saved-rank pooling
        confound (residual methods save rank-$2r$ adapters), so we do not quote the
        pooled rank correlation unqualified. This is not a generalization test of
        the relation.
  \item \textbf{Task and recipe breadth.} Two task types (commonsense, math) plus
        one bridging domain (MedMCQA); a single epoch under each method's faithful
        recipe. We do not claim universality across all instruction-tuning
        regimes.
  \item \textbf{Metrology: TruthfulQA sign-flip.} TruthfulQA \emph{rises} with
        forgetting on Llama and \emph{falls} on Qwen;
        because it sits inside \emph{broad} retention, including it attenuates the
        measured broad slope by $24$--$30\%$ on Llama. We
        report core retention (BBH, MMLU-Pro) as the headline and quote
        broad-without-TruthfulQA alongside. Llama's broad ceiling is calibrated
        ($35.26$); \emph{Qwen} broad-retention percentages remain for texture,
        pending Qwen base ceilings.
  \item \textbf{Seed structure and single-seed arms.} Seeds within a cell are
        correlated (residual ICC $\approx\!0.78$, per the verification pass's
        cluster analysis); all inference is cluster-robust or within-cell, and we
        never quote the naive OLS $F$. The campaign has $287$ cells with
        $n\!\ge\!3$ seeds across all six families; the Llama spine carries $3$--$5$
        seeds, while the geometry battery and some geometric-adapter operating
        points are single-seed, so per-method deltas against the geometric adapters
        stay at ``matches/edges.''
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility and Compute}\label{app:repro}
% ===========================================================================
Because our method \emph{is} a sweep, the run count is part of the claim. The
frozen analysis pool is \textbf{1{,}035 adapters} after quarantine filtering
(finite $\dw\!>\!0$ and finite retention; CorDA/CorDA++ withheld), spanning six
families: Llama-CS $180$, Llama-math $120$, Qwen-CS $151$, Qwen-math $164$,
Llama-CS grid $276$, Llama-math grid $144$ (seeds 42--46; $287$ cells have
$n\!\ge\!3$ seeds). Each cell is a full fine-tune followed by in-process
evaluation on the retention and adaptation suites. All numbers are computed from
the per-run \texttt{summary.json} store via one canonical loader
(\texttt{analysis\_final/}, the frozen ladder script), whose preflight reproduces
the frozen anchors (pooled $r\!=\!-0.847$, all six family cells) before emitting.
Geometry joins $1034/1035$; CE joins $911/1035$ (Qwen CE coverage
$\sim\!60\%$ from a method-balanced seed-block deletion, ignorable for the pooled
regressions and barred for per-seed Qwen CE). One byte-identical duplicate run
(a re-evaluation of a single grid cell) sits in the frozen $n\!=\!1035$; it enters
no regression, and the distinct-run count is $1{,}034$. The fresh audit analyses
(four-instruments observatory, correlation league, adjudication, insight
prospecting) are reproduced by the scripts under \texttt{acl\_analysis/}, each of
which hard-asserts the frozen $n\!=\!1035$ anchors before writing.

\paragraph{Estimator and registry mapping.} The axis of Equation~\ref{eq:fdelta}
is the registry field \texttt{fdelta}, computed in-process by forward pre-hooks on
every adapted module ($100$ prompts, batch $8$, max length $256$; tokens with
$\lVert x\rVert_2 \le 10^{-6}$ masked). For residual-initialized adapters
$\Delta W_\ell$ includes the initialization residual via a rank-$2r$ conversion,
so a $0$-step run measures $\Delta W = 0$ (self-check passed for all released
arms). $\dw$ beats the spectral norm ($R^2$ $0.72$ vs.\ $0.58$) and the Frobenius
norm ($0.56$); \texttt{dw\_sv\_max} and the geometry-pipeline \texttt{spec\_max}
are the same measurement ($r\!>\!0.9999$), cited once. The CE store mixes two
protocols (a 40-block slice and full WikiText-103); a protocol dummy leaves the KL
step unchanged, and per-family $r(\text{KL},\text{ret})$ agrees within $0.04$
across protocols---benign.

\paragraph{Compute.} All runs used single 8-GPU NVIDIA B200 nodes with one
training-plus-evaluation cell per GPU. We estimate the total campaign at
$\sim$1{,}700--2{,}000 B200-GPU-hours (from cell counts $\times$ per-cell times,
not metered).
