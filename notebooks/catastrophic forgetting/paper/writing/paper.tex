\begin{abstract}
% ===========================================================================
The literature ships a new parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapter almost
every week, each promising to mitigate catastrophic forgetting through a clever
\emph{geometric} prior: a data-aware initialization, a null-space projection, a
spectral decomposition, an orthogonality penalty. We argue that the
\emph{first-order} governing variable is the magnitude of the update, and that
geometry acts largely \emph{through} that magnitude rather than beside it, and we
make the case with two instruments
any adapter comparison needs: a method-neutral magnitude axis---the effective
update magnitude $\dw$ (the mean normalized output displacement the update induces,
CLoRA's $\mathbb{F}$ diagnostic), chosen over the spectral norm, which spiky
initializations inflate---and a per-method learning-rate sweep. Three claims
and a message follow. \textbf{(1) Mechanism:} forgetting is governed, to first
order, by $\dw$ rather than by adapter geometry---retention collapses onto a single
empirical curve
($r\!=\!-0.86$ pooled, $-0.92$ on the on-curve adapters), and adapter identity adds
essentially nothing once $\dw$ is controlled, with one provisional exception
(SC-LoRA, pending a calibration control). \textbf{(2) Diagnosis:} the elaborate
adapters' reported wins carry the \emph{ingredients} of a learning-rate
artifact---each peaks at a different learning rate, and the $\dw$ an adapter
produces predicts retention far better ($R^2\!=\!0.74$) than its learning rate does
($R^2\!=\!0.32$), so a per-method sweep collapses the apparent differences back
onto the shared curve. \textbf{(3) Consequence:} plain LoRA with a weight-decay
term---the simplest possible magnitude control---lands on the
adaptation--retention Pareto frontier of every elaborate geometric adapter,
matching or edging it by moving \emph{along} the shared curve, and does so with the
widest safe operating band in the study (6 of 7 learning rates versus SC-LoRA's 1
of 7). \textbf{Message:} control the size of the update first; adapter geometry
mostly sets \emph{where} on the magnitude curve a method starts, not a separate
axis of forgetting. The evidence is a
complete Llama-2-7B commonsense learning-rate grid (8 adapters $\times$ 7 rates
$=$ 56 cells, 49 clean after withholding CorDA), a sparse math grid, and a partial
Qwen2.5-7B replication (commonsense-LoRA replicates the law's sign; math-LoRA does
not yet). Even if the deferred controls later shift the rankings, the measurement
contribution stands: the fair $\dw$ axis and the sweep-as-instrument decompose any
adapter's apparent win into a magnitude effect plus a learning-rate-tuning
artifact.
\end{abstract}

% ===========================================================================
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================
Catastrophic forgetting---the erosion of a pretrained model's general
capabilities when it is fine-tuned on a narrow task---is one of the central
obstacles to deploying adapted large language models. Parameter-efficient
fine-tuning (PEFT), and in particular LoRA~\citep{hu2021lora} and its many
descendants, was supposed to help: by restricting the update to a low-rank
correction $\Delta W = BA$, one hopes to leave the bulk of the pretrained weights
untouched. Residual forgetting persists, and the community has answered it with a
steady stream of increasingly elaborate \emph{geometric} adapters. Some initialize
the adapter from the principal or minor singular directions of the pretrained
weight (PiSSA~\citep{meng2024pissa}, MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora}); some project
the update orthogonally to the dominant weight subspace
(OPLoRA~\citep{xiong2025oplora}); some take a data- or activation-covariance
decomposition that freezes a ``knowledge'' subspace (CorDA~\citep{yang2024corda},
SC-LoRA~\citep{luo2025sclora}); some add an orthogonality penalty against a frozen
subspace (CLoRA~\citep{clora}); some decompose magnitude from direction
(DoRA~\citep{liu2024dora}). Each is presented as a better way to choose
\emph{where} the update goes, and each reports a win over the last.

\paragraph{Our contribution.} This paper argues those wins carry the ingredients
of a learning-rate artifact. It makes one measurement contribution and one
empirical claim, and each stands on its own. The \emph{measurement contribution}
is a fair-comparison protocol for forgetting under PEFT: a method-neutral
magnitude axis---the effective update magnitude $\dw$ (CLoRA's $\mathbb{F}$
diagnostic, Section~\ref{sec:setup}), which spectral measures
confound because spiky initializations (CorDA, DoRA, SC-LoRA) inflate them
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}, Figure~\ref{fig:axis})---and a per-method learning-rate
sweep, which exposes what single-rate comparisons hide: different adapters
transmit the same learning rate into different $\dw$, so any fixed rate flatters
whichever method happens to be tuned there (Section~\ref{sec:lr}). The
\emph{empirical claim} is what the protocol reveals: forgetting is governed by
$\dw$, essentially independently of adapter geometry. Across 49 runs and seven
adapters, retention tracks $\log\dw$ at $r\!=\!-0.86$, and adding adapter identity
to the regression buys $\Delta R^2\!=\!0.13$, almost all of it from one
provisional outlier (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}). Two consequences follow.
\emph{Diagnostically}, geometry does not change the shape of the retention curve,
only where on the curve a given learning rate lands you---so single-rate wins
dissolve under the sweep (Section~\ref{sec:lr}). \emph{Practically}, the simplest
magnitude control---plain LoRA with weight decay---lands on the
adaptation--retention frontier of every elaborate adapter we swept, with the
widest safe learning-rate band in the study (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}). Deferred
controls (seeds, matched capacity, eval-matched calibration, a second model) can
shift the rankings; they cannot unmake the protocol or the curve.

With the two instruments in hand, the empirical spine of the paper is three claims
and a message: \textbf{(1) Mechanism} --- forgetting is governed to first order by
$\dw$, with adapter geometry acting largely through it (Section~\ref{sec:law},
Section~\ref{sec:geometry}); \textbf{(2) Diagnosis} --- the reported
per-method wins carry the ingredients of a learning-rate artifact that a
per-method sweep dissolves (Section~\ref{sec:lr}); \textbf{(3) Consequence} ---
plain LoRA{+}weight-decay lands on the adaptation--retention Pareto frontier of
every elaborate geometric adapter, as a corollary of the law
(Section~\ref{sec:pareto}); and the \textbf{message} --- control the size of the
update first; geometry is a fingerprint, not a separate lever. For coverage:
across the seven of eight adapters we can assess
(LoRA, LoRA-Null, LoRA{+}wd, DoRA, MiLoRA, CLoRA, SC-LoRA), six lie on a single
$\dw$ curve; SC-LoRA is the one provisional below-curve deviator, and CorDA is
withheld pending a calibration-fairness fix (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Relation to concurrent work.} Independently and from a different
codebase, \citet{lee2026lr} show that the peak \emph{adaptation} of nine LoRA
variants converges once each is tuned at its own learning rate. We establish the
retention-side counterpart: forgetting is governed by the magnitude that the
learning rate produces, and geometry adds essentially nothing beyond that magnitude
among the on-curve adapters (subspace alignment $\Delta R^2\!\approx\!0.0002$,
Section~\ref{sec:geometry})---corroborating their adaptation finding while extending
it to the axis that the forgetting-mitigation literature actually cares about.

\paragraph{Scope, stated once.} The Llama-2-7B commonsense evidence is complete:
the full $8\times 7$ learning-rate grid (49 clean cells; CorDA's 7 cells withheld,
Section~\ref{sec:limits}) on a single seed (seed~42). The math grid is sparse, and
the Qwen2.5-7B arm is a partial replication ($\sim$13 of $\sim$112 planned cells).
Section~\ref{sec:limits} states the gaps explicitly---single seed, unmatched
ranks, the calibration-versus-evaluation mismatch for the data-aware inits, and the
near-tautological character of the raw magnitude correlation.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}\label{sec:related}
% ===========================================================================
\paragraph{The adapter zoo, organized by basis.} The forgetting-mitigation
literature is best organized not by surface mechanism but by the single
load-bearing axis on which the methods actually differ: the \emph{basis} in which
they reason about which directions to protect.
\emph{(i) Static weight-SVD basis.} PiSSA~\citep{meng2024pissa} initializes from
the top singular triples of $W_0$; MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora} from the bottom
(minor) triples; OPLoRA~\citep{xiong2025oplora} projects the update into the
orthogonal complement of the top-$k$ singular subspace, provably preserving the
dominant singular triples. \emph{(ii) Data / activation-covariance basis.}
CorDA~\citep{yang2024corda} takes the SVD of $W_0\,\mathrm{Cov}_{\text{input}}$
over a calibration set and freezes the components most responsive to that data;
SC-LoRA~\citep{luo2025sclora} constructs an output subspace from a balance of task
and preservation covariances; LoRA-Null~\citep{tang2025loranull} initializes the
adapter in the null space of the input-activation covariance, so the initial
update avoids the directions the calibration data exercises;
CorDA++~\citep{yang2025cordapp} adds dynamic covariance selection and per-layer
rank allocation. \emph{(iii) Auxiliary loss /
other.} CLoRA~\citep{clora} penalizes the overlap of the adapter with a pre-defined
(e.g.\ random-orthonormal) subspace; DoRA~\citep{liu2024dora} decomposes each
column of the update into magnitude and direction. What every one of these shares
is a commitment to \emph{geometry}: the premise that \emph{where} the update lands
is the lever that controls forgetting. Our results say the lever is instead
\emph{how big} the update is.

\paragraph{Magnitude and forgetting, before and outside the adapter zoo.} The
idea that forgetting is controlled by how far the weights move is not new; what is
new is showing it is the \emph{whole} story once the measurement is fair. In the
pre-LLM transfer-learning literature, EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc} penalizes
Fisher-weighted deviation from the pretrained weights and
L$^2$-SP~\citep{li2018l2sp} recommends a plain $L^2$ penalty toward the starting
point---magnitude regularization with and without a geometric weighting. For LLM
fine-tuning, \citet{kalajdzievski2024scaling} fits scaling laws in which
forgetting grows as a shifted power law in the number of parameters tuned and the
number of update steps---coarse proxies for update size---and
\citet{biderman2024lora} show that LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning while
learning less, explicitly framing the two as coupled. Even inside the adapter zoo,
two recent methods report forgetting that moves with update size, in passing:
MiLoRA's own WikiText forgetting loss orders PiSSA${>}$LoRA${>}$MiLoRA (their
Table~8~\citep{wang2024milora}, using the CE-to-base metric
of~\citealp{kalajdzievski2024scaling}), tracking the update amplification they
tabulate, and LoRA-Null's Table~4b shows CorDA's retention collapsing
($89\%{\to}73\%$) as its rank---hence its update capacity---grows
\citep{tang2025loranull}. Both are consistent with the magnitude law, but each is
reported for one method's own design. None of these works measures
$\dw$ directly across methods, compares adapter geometries on a common magnitude
axis, or sweeps the learning rate per method---so none can ask whether geometry adds
anything \emph{beyond} magnitude, which is our question.

One point of apparent tension deserves a direct answer.
\citet{biderman2024lora} also tested weight decay as a forgetting mitigation for
\emph{full fine-tuning} and found it learned and forgot like the unregularized
baseline---seemingly the opposite of our Claim~3. The two results are consistent
under the magnitude law: they applied small coefficients ($5\times10^{-5}$,
$10^{-4}$) to all seven billion parameters at a single previously-tuned learning
rate, a setting in which the penalty barely moves $\dw$; we apply weight decay
$0.3$ to the adapter matrices alone under a per-method rate sweep, which
demonstrably walks the model down the retention curve
(Section~\ref{sec:pareto}). Indeed, their single-rate design is itself an
instance of the comparison our Claim~2 is about: without a per-method sweep, a
regularizer's effect on the adaptation--retention trade-off cannot be separated
from its effect on the effective learning rate.

\paragraph{Why magnitude is overlooked in the adapter literature.} Weight decay is
the oldest, simplest knob in deep learning, and it acts directly on the \emph{size}
of the update rather than its direction. Precisely because it carries no geometric
story, it is almost never the protagonist of an adapter paper. Within the
PEFT-adapter literature, the prior work that most explicitly connects update
magnitude to forgetting is CLoRA, whose penalty can be read as an indirect
magnitude control. Our contribution is therefore \emph{not} the discovery that
magnitude matters. It is (i) that magnitude is the \emph{method-free} governing
variable; (ii) that once $\dw$ is controlled, subspace geometry adds essentially
nothing measurable ($\Delta R^2\!\approx\!0.0002$; Section~\ref{sec:geometry})---a
claim no single-method paper such as CLoRA, no
regularization study such as \citet{biderman2024lora} or
\citet{kalajdzievski2024scaling} (neither of which places competing geometries on
a controlled-$\dw$ axis), and no adaptation-only study such as \citet{lee2026lr}
(who link the differing optimal learning rates to the largest Hessian eigenvalue
but do not study retention), is positioned to make; and (iii) that the reported
per-method wins carry the ingredients of a learning-rate artifact that a
per-method sweep dissolves (Section~\ref{sec:lr}).

\section{Setup and Measurement}\label{sec:setup}
% ===========================================================================
\paragraph{Models and domains.} Our primary, complete evidence is on
\textbf{Llama-2-7B}; a replication on \textbf{Qwen2.5-7B} is partial. We fine-tune
on two domains: \emph{commonsense} (the eight-task LLM-Adapters commonsense
suite~\citep{hu2023llmadapters}) and \emph{math} (trained on MetaMathQA, evaluated
on GSM8K).

\paragraph{Why a common harness is a prerequisite, not a convenience.} A fair
cross-method comparison of \emph{forgetting} cannot be assembled from the published
numbers, because the adapter papers do not measure retention on a common axis.
Among the competitors, \emph{only} CLoRA~\citep{clora} reports our retention
benchmarks (BBH and MMLU-Pro); the data-aware inits report closed-book factoid-QA
exact-match (CorDA, SC-LoRA, and LoRA-Null on TriviaQA/NQ/WebQS), MiLoRA reports
only a WikiText loss proxy, and PiSSA and DoRA report no forgetting metric at all.
Their ``retention'' numbers therefore cannot be placed on a single axis. Evaluating
every adapter ourselves under one identical harness is thus not a matter of
convenience but a precondition for any fair forgetting comparison---and is itself
part of the measurement contribution.

\paragraph{The adapters, as one trainer.} A key design decision makes the
comparison fair: every structured adapter is implemented as ``LoRA with a different
initialization'' loaded into a single shared PEFT LoRA trainer, so that the only
difference between arms is the init routine---extracted as $(A_{\text{init}},
B_{\text{init}}, W_{\text{residual}})$---and, for CLoRA alone, one added loss term.
We study eight arms: plain \textbf{LoRA} (r16), \textbf{LoRA{+}wd} (r32, weight
decay 0.3 on the adapter matrices only), \textbf{DoRA} (r16), \textbf{CorDA} (r16,
KPA retention mode, nq\_open calibration), \textbf{MiLoRA} (r32), \textbf{SC-LoRA}
(r32, $\beta{=}0.5$), \textbf{LoRA-Null} (r16), and \textbf{CLoRA} ($k{=}1024$).
LoRA-Null~\citep{tang2025loranull} initializes the adapter from the null space of
the input-activation covariance---calibrated, like CorDA and SC-LoRA, on
nq\_open---so that the initial update avoids the directions the calibration data
exercises. Two members of the static weight-SVD family are positioned in
Section~\ref{sec:related} but not swept: PiSSA, whose principal-triple
initialization targets adaptation speed rather than retention (MiLoRA is that
family's retention-oriented member and represents it here), and OPLoRA, whose
projection protects exactly the weight-SVD subspace that our companion
direction diagnostics find uninformative for retention
(Section~\ref{sec:law}).
Before claiming any method ``fails,'' we audited each port against its reference
implementation; all ports are faithful, and the residual-init methods (MiLoRA,
SC-LoRA) pass a 0-step self-check that a $\Delta W = 0$ update recovers base
retention. CorDA did \emph{not} pass a clean self-check under the current
calibration and is withheld (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Ranks are \emph{not}
matched across arms (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), and one further asymmetry is
inherent to the residual construction: the plain arms use scaling $\alpha\!=\!2r$,
whereas the residual-init arms (CorDA, MiLoRA, SC-LoRA, LoRA-Null) are forced to
$\alpha\!=\!r$ by the rank-$2r$ residual conversion, so a fixed nominal learning
rate maps to a different update growth per family. The $\dw$ reparametrization
absorbs this: it shifts a method \emph{along} the learning-rate grid, not off the
retention curve.

\paragraph{Scale validation.} Our accuracy scale reproduces the canonical
Llama-2-7B LoRA anchor. Run at the canonical LLM-Adapters commonsense learning rate
($3\text{e-}4$)~\citep{hu2023llmadapters}, our plain LoRA scores BoolQ $69.97$
against the canonical $69.8$ (DoRA's reproduction under the LLM-Adapters
recipe~\citep{liu2024dora}), and its eight-task CS-8 accuracy of $79.1$ sits
between the two published Llama-2-7B LoRA baselines reported under that
recipe---DoRA's $77.6$ and CLoRA's $79.9$~\citep{clora}. Our accuracies are
therefore on the established scale for this setup, so the retention numbers we
report are trustworthy in absolute terms.

\paragraph{Learning-rate sweep.} We sweep seven learning rates per method,
$\{2\text{e-}5, 5\text{e-}5, 1\text{e-}4, 2\text{e-}4, 3\text{e-}4, 5\text{e-}4,
1\text{e-}3\}$. The two highest rates originally in the grid (2e-3, 5e-3) diverged
to NaN weights on every method and are dropped; the forgetting regime is already
reached at 1e-3. The per-method sweep is the paper's central instrument
(Section~\ref{sec:lr}).

\paragraph{Retention and adaptation metrics.} \emph{Retention (core)} is the mean
of BBH (answer-only, 3-shot) and MMLU-Pro (5-shot CoT); the no-fine-tuning base
ceiling is $33.10$ and $18.96$ respectively, for a core ceiling of $\mathbf{26.0}$.
\emph{Retention (broad)} adds MMLU, ARC-Challenge, and TruthfulQA; because the base
ceilings for these three are not yet calibrated, broad-retention numbers are
reported \emph{for texture only, uncalibrated}. \emph{Adaptation} is eight-task
commonsense accuracy (CS-8) or GSM8K exact-match (math).

%eval-only runs); broad-retention percentages and their slopes are not normalizable.

\paragraph{The magnitude axis $\dw$.} Our governing variable is the
\textbf{effective update magnitude}: the mean normalized output displacement the
update induces on real token traffic, which is exactly CLoRA's $\mathbb{F}$
forgetting diagnostic~\citep{clora} (their Eq.~3). Let $\mathcal{L}$ be
the set of adapted weight matrices, let $\Delta W_\ell$ be module $\ell$'s total
weight update (with any initialization residual included, so that a $0$-step run
gives $\Delta W_\ell = 0$), and let $x_{\ell,t}$ be the input activation of token
$t$ at module $\ell$, captured by forward hooks while running a fixed batch of 100
held-out commonsense evaluation prompts---the same probe set for every run in both
domains, so the measure is comparable across all cells. With $N_\ell$ the
number of tokens reaching module $\ell$, we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fdelta}
  \dw \;=\; \frac{1}{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}} N_\ell}
  \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}} \sum_{t=1}^{N_\ell}
  \frac{\lVert \Delta W_\ell\, x_{\ell,t} \rVert_2}{\lVert x_{\ell,t} \rVert_2}.
\end{equation}
Each term is the normalized output displacement the update causes on real token
traffic; for isotropic inputs its square concentrates on
$\lVert\Delta W_\ell\rVert_F^2 / d_{\text{in}}$ in the high-dimensional limit (as a
ratio of expectations, where $\lVert x\rVert_2^2$ concentrates), so it behaves like a
per-dimension, token-weighted Frobenius norm while being defined as an effective
\emph{output displacement} rather than a raw matrix norm. We aggregate it
token-weighted over all module--token pairs rather than averaging per matrix, so our
$\dw$ values live on the same normalized scale as---and are directly comparable
to---CLoRA's Table~4 (their LoRA $0.79$, LoRA-L2 $0.29$, $k$2048 $0.14$; our spectral
fields $\smax$/$\bar\sigma$ correspond instead to their $\lVert\Delta W\rVert$
column);
values range $\sim\!0.05$--$3.7$ on the clean sweep (estimator details and
registry-field mapping in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
We adopt this measure over the maximum spectral norm $\smax$ because $\smax$ is
method-confounded: spiky inits (CorDA, DoRA, SC-LoRA) inflate it, which would
mis-rank them. Figure~\ref{fig:axis} shows that among three candidate magnitude
measures ($\dw$, mean spectral norm, and $\smax$), $\dw$ gives the tightest, most
method-neutral fit---it explains $R^2\!=\!0.74$ of the variance in retention
versus $0.36$ for the mean spectral norm and $0.33$ for $\smax$---so it is the fair
$x$-axis for the whole paper. All pooled figures aggregate seven series---LoRA, LoRA-Null,
LoRA{+}wd, DoRA, MiLoRA, CLoRA, SC-LoRA---for
$n\!=\!49\!=\!7\text{ series}\times7\text{ LR}$; CorDA is withheld
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{The Magnitude Law}\label{sec:law}
% ===========================================================================
Our central result is an empirical regularity we call the magnitude law (``law''
in the descriptive, not the axiomatic, sense): retention is a tight, monotone,
\emph{method-independent} function of $\dw$. Figure~\ref{fig:hero} is the central
exhibit: plotting core retention against $\dw$ (log scale) for all adapters,
points from six of the seven series interleave along one downward curve (the sole
provisional exception, SC-LoRA, is ringed and discussed below).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig0_hero.png}
  \caption{\textbf{The magnitude law.} Core retention (mean of BBH and MMLU-Pro)
  versus $\dw$ (log scale), Llama-2-7B commonsense, seed 42, seven adapter series.
  Points from six of the seven series interleave along one curve. Black line:
  pooled fit on the six on-curve adapters with a 95\% bootstrap CI
  ($r\!=\!-0.92$, $R^2\!=\!0.84$, slope $-10.0$\,pp/decade, $n\!=\!42$);
  all-adapters pooled fit $r\!=\!-0.86$, slope $-14.8$\,pp/decade ($n\!=\!49$).
  SC-LoRA is ringed as the single below-curve deviator (provisional;
  Section~\ref{sec:limits}). The star marks the LoRA{+}wd best-adapt point
  (Table~\ref{tab:cs}): the practical punchline of Section~\ref{sec:pareto} is
  that the simplest magnitude control sits at the low-$\dw$, high-retention end
  of this same curve. Green dotted line: base ceiling (26.0).}
  \label{fig:hero}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The correlations.} On Llama-2 commonsense, retention is a tight,
monotone, method-independent function of $\dw$ in which the methods
\emph{interleave}: the pooled correlation with $\log_{10}\dw$ is $r\!=\!-0.86$
($R^2\!=\!0.74$, slope $-14.8$\,pp/decade, $n\!=\!49$,
$p\!=\!3\!\times\!10^{-15}$), the six on-curve adapters tighten it to
$r\!=\!-0.92$ ($R^2\!=\!0.84$, slope $-10.0$\,pp/decade, $n\!=\!42$), and
\emph{every adapter individually} traces the same line, with within-method
correlations from $-0.86$ to $-0.97$ (plain LoRA $-0.95$, LoRA-Null $-0.86$,
LoRA{+}wd $-0.89$, DoRA $-0.97$, MiLoRA $-0.94$, CLoRA $-0.90$, SC-LoRA $-0.97$)
rather than method-specific sub-curves.

\paragraph{The relationship is monotone and saturating, not a linear artifact.} A
rank-based Spearman correlation is $\rho\!=\!-0.90$ ($p\!=\!3.5\times10^{-18}$),
slightly stronger than the Pearson value, so the monotonicity does not depend on the
linear parametrization. The functional form is in fact a saturating one: a
hockey-stick fit (a floored linear decline) beats both a straight line and a
quadratic on AIC \emph{and} on leave-one-out cross-validation, with a knee at
$\dw\!\approx\!0.36$---almost exactly LoRA{+}wd's operating point
(Section~\ref{sec:pareto})---and a fitted asymptote ($26.8$) essentially at the
base ceiling ($26.0$);
the quadratic is the worst out-of-sample because its upturn at small $\dw$ is
unphysical. Below the ceiling (retention $<25$, where the curve is not floored) the
slope steepens to $-21$\,pp/decade (Tobit $-22$ accounting for censoring), so the
pooled $-14.8$ if anything \emph{understates} the true cost of magnitude. Finally,
the partial correlation of retention with $\log\dw$ \emph{controlling for method
identity} is $-0.87$, and a method-label permutation test rejects the null at
$p\!<\!5\times10^{-5}$: the association is between magnitude and retention, not an
artifact of which methods happen to sit where.

\paragraph{Second-model evidence (preliminary).} To test whether the law is a
Llama idiosyncrasy we are replicating the design on Qwen2.5-7B; this arm is early
($\sim$13 of $\sim$112 planned cells, mostly LoRA). On the slice available, the
commonsense LoRA sweep replicates the law's sign and monotonicity ($r\!=\!-0.88$ on
core retention, $-0.92$ on the broad metric; $n\!=\!7$, $p\!=\!9\!\times\!10^{-3}$),
though at a steeper slope ($-34.8$\,pp/decade vs Llama's $-10$ to $-15$)---so this
replicates the \emph{sign and monotonicity}, not yet the quantitative law. The Qwen
math LoRA sweep does \emph{not} yet replicate ($r\!=\!+0.67$, n.s., $p\!=\!0.21$):
the higher-learning-rate cells that reach the forgetting regime are not yet run. We
report this anti-replication openly and draw no Qwen ranking.

forgets on both benchmarks relative to base (BBH $34.91\!\to\!26.69$, MMLU-Pro

numbers up one-to-one: CLoRA's base BBH ($34.91$) exceeds our answer-only base
($33.10$), a harness-configuration gap, so we read CLoRA as independent
triangulation---relative degradation from each paper's own base points the same
way---rather than as a shared coordinate system (Appendix~\ref{app:clora}).

\section{Geometry Is a Second-Order Fingerprint, Not a Separate Lever}
\label{sec:geometry}\label{sec:ancova}
% ===========================================================================
The central negative result of this paper is that adapter \emph{geometry}---the
subspace an initialization or penalty steers the update into---adds essentially
nothing to retention once the update magnitude $\dw$ is held fixed. A subspace
alignment term buys $\Delta R^2\!\approx\!0.0002$ ($p\!=\!0.53$) beyond $\dw$ and
method identity, and the strongest candidate ``geometric axis''---concentration of
the update in the base weight's principal singular directions---\emph{flips sign}
the moment its two principal-space-init outliers are removed
(Section~\ref{sec:fingerprint}). We report this as \emph{rigor, not retraction}: we
looked hard for a second, geometric axis of forgetting, testing it directly on
all 320 saved adapters, and did not find one. What survives is constructive---the
same measurements that rule geometry out as a \emph{lever} make it an excellent
\emph{fingerprint}, one that recovers each method's advertised design from its
trained weights and explains the study's two below-curve outliers mechanistically.

This is the result that separates our claim from everything prior. The raw
correlation ``a bigger update forgets more'' is near-tautological, so we do not rest
the claim on it; the non-trivial, falsifiable statement is that \emph{adapter
identity adds essentially nothing beyond $\dw$}. No prior method comparison is
positioned to make it: CLoRA argues from a single method, and the concurrent
adaptation study of \citet{lee2026lr} measures task accuracy, not retention. We
test it three ways.

\emph{Intercepts (ANCOVA).} A pooled log-linear fit gives $R^2\!=\!0.74$; adding
per-method intercepts raises it to $R^2\!=\!0.87$
(Figure~\ref{fig:fairness})---an improvement that is significant overall
($F(6,41)\!=\!7.1$, $p\!<\!0.001$) but driven almost entirely by a single method.
The per-method residuals from the pooled spline curve are $+0.99$\,pp (LoRA),
$+0.60$ (LoRA-Null), $+0.06$ (LoRA{+}wd), $+1.04$ (MiLoRA), $+0.09$ (CLoRA), and
$+1.37$ (DoRA)---all statistically indistinguishable from zero---and $-4.15$\,pp
for SC-LoRA ($p\!=\!0.006$), the sole significant deviator. Restricted to the six
on-curve adapters, the intercepts are jointly non-significant
($F(5,35)\!=\!1.79$, $p\!=\!0.14$).

\emph{Slopes.} Geometry could in principle bend the curve rather than shift it,
so we also test per-method slopes. Among the six on-curve adapters the slopes are
statistically indistinguishable ($F(5,30)\!=\!0.28$, $p\!=\!0.92$; per-method
slopes $-8.1$ to $-11.7$\,pp/decade). Over all seven series the slope interaction
is significant ($F(6,35)\!=\!9.3$, $p\!<\!0.001$), again driven entirely by
SC-LoRA (slope $-26.0$): the one provisional deviator differs in both level and
slope, and the other six share one line.

\emph{Prediction (leave-one-method-out).} Finally, we ask whether the pooled law
\emph{predicts} an adapter it has never seen. Fitting the law on six series and
predicting the seventh yields held-out RMSEs of $1.7$--$3.6$\,pp (mean $2.5$)
across the six on-curve adapters---about the \emph{in-sample} accuracy of a
method-aware model with seven free intercepts ($2.2$\,pp). Held-out SC-LoRA is
mispredicted by $-7.4$\,pp on average (RMSE $9.1$), consistent with its
provisional below-curve status.

\emph{An independent forgetting metric agrees.} The three tests above all live on
our retention benchmarks; a fourth check uses a wholly different axis. We compute
each adapter's cross-entropy-to-base---the soft CE between the fine-tuned and base
next-token distributions on WikiText-103, the forgetting proxy MiLoRA report in
their Table~8 following \citet{kalajdzievski2024scaling}. It reproduces MiLoRA's
published ordering (our plain LoRA $3.57$ against their $3.24$; PiSSA $6.31$ against
their $6.07$; PiSSA${>}$LoRA), and it rises monotonically with $\dw$ (Spearman
$\rho\!=\!0.94$). The decisive comparison is that at a \emph{matched} learning rate
($3\text{e-}4$) and rank, MiLoRA's CE-to-base ($3.66$) is indistinguishable from
plain LoRA's ($3.57$): MiLoRA does not forget less once magnitude is held fixed, and
its reported advantage traces to a lower-magnitude operating point rather than to the
minor-singular subspace---the magnitude law confirmed on a metric we did not design.

\paragraph{No residual geometric axis survives the stress test.} A natural worry is
that some \emph{direction} of the update---not just its size---carries retention. We
tested the strongest candidate: the concentration of the update in the base weight's
top (principal) singular directions, via an SVD-alignment battery over all 320 saved
adapters (Section~\ref{sec:fingerprint}). The preliminary partial correlations
looked promising ($-0.48$ to $-0.58$ between principal alignment and retention once
$\dw$ is regressed out), but they are carried \emph{entirely} by two
principal-space-init methods (PiSSA and SC-LoRA): drop those two and the correlation
\emph{flips sign} (to $+0.3$ to $+0.4$ across the alignment metrics), and among the
on-curve adapters alone it is near zero ($-0.06$ to $-0.14$, n.s.). Added to a model that already contains $\dw$ and method identity,
the alignment term buys $\Delta R^2\!=\!0.0002$ ($p\!=\!0.53$). The independent
CE-to-base check agrees: MiLoRA (minor-space init) forgets like plain LoRA at matched
magnitude (above). There is no second, geometric axis of forgetting once magnitude
is controlled.

\paragraph{Resolving the tension: geometry sets \emph{where} on the axis, not a
second axis.} Two facts sit side by side---geometry adds nothing measurable among
magnitude-matched adapters, yet the two most geometrically distinctive methods
(PiSSA, SC-LoRA) are exactly the ones that fall below the curve---and they are not in
conflict. Geometry acts by setting \emph{where on the magnitude axis a method
starts}, not by adding a dimension orthogonal to it. A principal-space
initialization begins training aligned with the directions the loss gradient most
excites, so at a fixed learning rate it accumulates a \emph{larger, more
mis-allocated} update: it forgets more because it moves further, not because
``principal'' is intrinsically fragile. Among adapters that reach the same $\dw$,
the subspace they occupy is immaterial to retention. This is the load-bearing
reconciliation of the paper: the axis worth controlling is a \emph{magnitude}, and
geometry is a \emph{fingerprint} of how a method lands on it---which the rest of this
section makes quantitative.

\subsection{Geometry as a fingerprint}\label{sec:fingerprint}
The same SVD-alignment metrics that fail as a \emph{predictor} succeed as a
\emph{diagnostic}. Reconstructing each trained update $\Delta W=(\alpha/r)BA$ from the
320 saved adapters (no retraining) and projecting it onto the base weight's own
singular subspaces recovers, layer by layer, what each method was designed to
do---and the signature persists through three epochs of training
(Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}). MiLoRA is the only arm whose update energy leans toward
the base weight's \emph{minor} singular directions ($e_{\rm bot}\!=\!0.115$ vs.\
$e_{\rm top}\!=\!0.067$), its stated minor-init design; LoRA-Null concentrates on the
output-side principal directions ($e_{\rm top}\!=\!0.126$); SC-LoRA spikes on the
\emph{input-side} principal directions ($e^{\rm in}_{\rm top}\!=\!0.41$ on average,
exceeding $0.9$ in the early $q/k$ projections at low learning rate); while plain LoRA,
LoRA{+}wd, CLoRA, and DoRA sit near the $\approx\!0.06$ random-alignment baseline. The
fingerprint pays off by explaining the study's two below-curve outliers
mechanistically rather than by positing a geometric law. SC-LoRA's input-principal
concentration is precisely the erosion its own paper warns of: it \emph{decays} as the
learning rate rises ($e^{\rm in}_{\rm top}:\,0.70\!\to\!0.21$, $r\!=\!-0.96$;
Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}D), so at the rate where it finally adapts it has already
spent its constraint and sits $4.15$\,pp below the law---the very deviation the
ANCOVA above flags as the one significant residual. PiSSA (principal init) is the
study's worst forgetter for the same reason.

\paragraph{Rank is a modest second lever; static-SVD direction is not.} Two further
observations locate what does and does not matter beyond $\dw$. \emph{(i)} Rank acts
largely \emph{through} $\dw$---raising the LoRA rank raises $\dw$ (with $\sigma_1$
growing) and drives retention down---but a residual effect survives at matched
magnitude: the partial correlation of retention with rank given $\dw$ is $-0.56$,
itself largely collinear with the update's stable rank. So magnitude is first and
rank a modest second, and the folklore that low rank alone mitigates forgetting
largely folds into the magnitude law. \emph{(ii)} The direction of the update in the
static weight-SVD basis---a leakage thermometer $\mu_E$ (the output leak into the
preserved subspace), exactly the quantity the static-SVD adapters (PiSSA, MiLoRA,
OPLoRA) reason about---predicts retention at $r\!\approx\!-0.09$, i.e.\ not at all.
The lever that family optimizes is uninformative for retention once magnitude is
fixed.\footnote{The LoRA rank sweep and the $\mu_E$ leakage measurement are companion
experiments not in the released summary registry; the 320-adapter drift battery is
computed from the saved adapter weights (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).}

\paragraph{CorDA appears here only as a fingerprint.} CorDA's own signature---an
input-side \emph{minor}-direction spike ($e^{\rm in}_{\rm bot}\!=\!0.49$, in the MLP
down-projections) together with a large-magnitude blow-up---is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}C for completeness, but CorDA is \emph{excluded from every
quantitative claim in this paper} (law fit, Pareto table, ANCOVA residual): it is
mis-calibrated under the current setup and its $\dw$ explodes, so its position
carries no on/off-law meaning (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). It is a fingerprint, not an
assessed data point.

Six of the seven assessed adapters are on the law. We treat the SC-LoRA deviation
as \emph{provisional}: it is confounded by the calibration-versus-evaluation
mismatch and rests on a single seed (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), so we ring it as
an open question rather than headline it as a geometric effect. The summary
measurement stands: an in-sample $R^2$ gain of only $0.13$ from six free
per-method intercepts---with intercepts, slopes, and out-of-sample prediction all
indistinguishable across the on-curve adapters---plus the $\Delta R^2\!=\!0.0002$
subspace-alignment null, is the direct measurement of how little geometry buys once
magnitude is held fixed.

\section{Learning Rate Is Only a Proxy}\label{sec:lr}
% ===========================================================================
\emph{Geometry does not change the shape of the retention curve; it changes only
where on the curve a given learning rate lands you.} That sentence is the
diagnosis (Claim~2), and this section establishes it. If forgetting is governed by
$\dw$, then learning rate should matter only insofar as it sets $\dw$---and,
critically, different adapters should transmit the \emph{same} learning rate into
\emph{different} $\dw$. Both hold, and together they are the mechanism of the
reported ``wins.''

\paragraph{$\dw$ beats learning rate as a predictor.} Regressing retention on
$\log(\text{learning rate})$ gives $R^2\!=\!0.32$ ($r\!=\!-0.57$); regressing it on
$\log(\dw)$ gives $R^2\!=\!0.74$ ($r\!=\!-0.86$). The variance explained more than
doubles when the $x$-axis switches from the learning rate to the update magnitude it
produces (Figure~\ref{fig:proxy}). Because a data-aware init starts the adapter
where the loss gradient is large, at a fixed learning rate it accumulates a
\emph{larger} $\dw$ than a vanilla init (Figure~\ref{fig:proxy}, panel A); geometry
does not change the \emph{shape} of the retention curve, it changes \emph{where on
the curve} a given learning rate lands you.

  \caption{\textbf{Learning rate is only a proxy; $\dw$ is what governs retention.} Panel A: the
  same learning rate maps to different $\dw$ depending on the init (data-aware inits

\paragraph{The best learning rate is not shared.} Each method peaks at a different
learning rate: SC-LoRA at 5e-5, DoRA at 2e-4, LoRA and MiLoRA at 3e-4, LoRA{+}wd,
LoRA-Null, and CLoRA at 5e-4 (Figure~\ref{fig:lrsens}, Appendix~\ref{app:figs}).\footnote{These
best-adapt rates use the unconstrained ``max adaptation'' rule of Table~\ref{tab:cs};
the LR-artifact comparison in Table~\ref{tab:lr_artifact} uses a
retention-constrained rule (max adaptation subject to core retention $\ge24$), which
is why SC-LoRA's best-looking rate appears there as 2e-5 (its 5e-5 peak sits below
the retention floor) rather than 5e-5.} A single fixed learning rate therefore
flatters whichever method happens to be well tuned at that rate---precisely the
mechanism by which single-LR comparisons produce ``wins,'' and what our per-method
sweep is built to neutralize.

\paragraph{The competitor comparisons fix one rate.} This is not a hypothetical
failure mode. Most of the adapter comparisons we reproduce hold the learning rate
fixed and untuned across all methods, and state so: MiLoRA~\citep{wang2024milora},
CLoRA~\citep{clora}, LoRA-Null~\citep{tang2025loranull}, and
CorDA++~\citep{yang2025cordapp} each use a single shared learning rate for every arm
(MiLoRA and CLoRA inherit the LLM-Adapters commonsense rate; LoRA-Null and CorDA++
fix $2\text{e-}5$ for all methods), and none reports the $\dw$ that rate produces.
SC-LoRA~\citep{luo2025sclora} is the partial exception---it tuned its baselines'
learning rate in two of its three experiments---but even there its structured arm is
held fixed. A per-method sweep with $\dw$ logged is thus exactly the control this
literature omits.

\paragraph{The single-LR illusion, made explicit.} We can now show the illusion
directly rather than assert it (Figure~\ref{fig:lrartifact},
Table~\ref{tab:lr_artifact}). For each structured adapter we take its
\emph{best-looking single learning rate}---the rate that maximizes commonsense
adaptation while keeping core retention $\geq\!24$---and compare it against plain
LoRA \emph{run at that same rate}. At that fixed rate several methods do look better
than LoRA: SC-LoRA by $+26.0$\,pp of adaptation, LoRA-Null by $+19.5$, DoRA by
$+5.9$, MiLoRA by $+0.8$ (the illusion is strong for the low-LR-optimal data-aware
inits, weak for MiLoRA, and \emph{absent} for CLoRA, which is $-14.2$ even at its own
best rate). But once the learning rate is swept, the LoRA{+}wd frontier sits on or
above every one of these best single-LR points on \emph{both} axes in this
single-seed sweep (Table~\ref{tab:lr_artifact}). The retention margins here are
sub-1\,pp and within single-seed noise (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); the
load-bearing point is not the size of the gap but that \emph{no fixed-LR advantage
survives a symmetric sweep}. The apparent per-method win thus decomposes into a
learning-rate-tuning effect that vanishes under the sweep, plus a residual that
lands back on the shared magnitude curve. This is the concrete, per-method form of
Claim~2.

\section{The Practical Corollary: Weight Decay Bounds $\dw$ for Free}\label{sec:pareto}
% ===========================================================================
The magnitude law has a deflationary practical corollary. If $\dw$ is the lever,
then the simplest possible control of it---a weight-decay term on the adapter
matrices, with no geometric machinery at all---should land on the frontier of the
elaborate adapters. It does, and the way it does so is the point:
\textbf{LoRA{+}wd wins by moving \emph{along} the shared curve to a smaller
magnitude, not by beating the curve.} Its ANCOVA residual is $+0.06$\,pp---dead on
the law (Section~\ref{sec:ancova}).

\paragraph{The mechanism: a two-edged budget.} $\dw$ is a budget with two edges.
Adaptation \emph{rises} with $\dw$ (at $+20.3$\,pp/decade) while retention
\emph{falls} (at $-14.8$\,pp/decade), so there is a band of $\dw$ in which
adaptation is near its maximum while retention stays near the base ceiling
(Figure~\ref{fig:budget}). Adapters fall off the frontier when their
(init~$\times$~learning-rate) combination pushes $\dw$ past the right edge of that
band. Weight decay penalizes $\lVert A\rVert^2 + \lVert B\rVert^2$ regardless of
subspace, so it \emph{bounds} $\dw$ and lands the model inside the band ``for
free'': LoRA{+}wd(0.3) carries the smallest update in the comparison
($\dw\!=\!0.394$), while the un-regularized high-adapters sit at larger magnitudes.
The ``for free'' is literal on compute as well as engineering---weight decay is a
single optimizer flag that adds no initialization pass and leaves training wall-clock
indistinguishable from plain LoRA, whereas the geometric adapters pay a
data-aware-initialization or per-step regularizer tax for the same trainable budget
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
That is the whole mechanism of Claim~3---the geometric adapters are elaborate ways
to choose \emph{where} the update goes; weight decay is the trivial way to choose
\emph{how big} it is, and how big is what matters.

\paragraph{On the frontier, both domains.} Table~\ref{tab:cs} reports each method at
its own adaptation-optimal learning rate on commonsense (the fair, per-method-tuned
comparison), and Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} plots the plane. LoRA{+}wd(0.3) at lr5e-4

confirms it sparsely: LoRA{+}wd(0.3) at lr5e-4 reaches GSM8K $=\!50.6$ / retention

\paragraph{Robustness, not just the peak.} The single-seed peak comparison is the
noisiest part of the evidence; the robustness column of Table~\ref{tab:cs} is the

rather than resting on any single cell. Bounding $\dw$ does not just move the best
point---it widens the band of learning rates that are safe at all: LoRA{+}wd keeps
core retention $\geq\!24$ at \textbf{6 of 7} learning rates (plain LoRA, LoRA-Null,
MiLoRA, and CLoRA 5 of 7; DoRA 4 of 7), whereas SC-LoRA does so at \textbf{1 of 7}.

sweep and almost nowhere on SC-LoRA's (full operating points:

\paragraph{The honest verb, and why the margin is not the point.} We claim that
LoRA{+}wd \emph{matches or edges} the frontier at far lower engineering cost---not
that it \emph{dominates}. An earlier full-scale read that looked like domination
(``LoRA{+}wd dominates CLoRA'') deflated to a tie once a checkpoint-collision data
bug was fixed. On a single seed the frontier is noisy and non-monotone, and the
LoRA{+}wd margin is smaller than the documented seed variance
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). But under the corollary framing this does not weaken the
claim: the result is not that LoRA{+}wd \emph{ranks first}, it is that a
subspace-free magnitude knob \emph{reaches the frontier at all}, which is exactly
what the magnitude law predicts and what the geometric machinery was supposed to be
necessary for.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================
The field is optimizing a second-order variable. Catastrophic forgetting under PEFT
is governed to first order by a single scalar, the magnitude of the weight update
$\dw$; geometry---the choice of \emph{where} the update goes---acts largely
\emph{through} that magnitude and, once it is controlled, adds essentially nothing
measurable beyond it ($\Delta R^2\!\approx\!0.0002$ for subspace alignment, with one
provisional exception; Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). The diagnosis exhibits the
\emph{ingredients} of a learning-rate artifact behind the reported wins: each
adapter peaks at a different rate, and the $\dw$ it produces predicts retention
far better than its learning rate does, so a per-method sweep collapses the
difference back onto the shared curve. The practical consequence is deflationary:
plain LoRA with a weight-decay term matches or edges the adaptation--retention
frontier of every elaborate geometric adapter we tested---and holds the widest
safe learning-rate band while doing so---not by ranking first but by reaching a
frontier the geometric machinery was supposed to be necessary for; it simply moves
along the shared curve to a smaller magnitude.

Each claim is stated at the strength its evidence supports. The law (Claim~1) is
complete and robust on Llama-2. The diagnosis and consequence (Claims~2 and~3) are
supported at the honest strength of ``best-LR-is-not-shared'' and
``matches/edges,'' and become fully airtight only once the deferred controls land:
seeds on the headline cells, a param-matched LoRA{+}wd control, an eval-matched
calibration for the data-aware inits, CorDA re-instated (or openly declared
off-curve), and the completed Qwen $2\times2$. Should those controls change the
rankings, the paper still stands as a \emph{measurement} contribution: the
learning-rate-sweep-per-method decomposes any adapter's reported win into its true
magnitude effect plus a learning-rate-tuning artifact, and the
effective-update-magnitude axis $\dw$ is the fair way to compare adapters.

\paragraph{Future work.} Beyond the controls in Section~\ref{sec:limits}, three
directions follow naturally. \emph{(i) CorDA++}~\citep{yang2025cordapp} (dynamic
covariance selection and dynamic rank allocation under a fixed parameter budget)
is the strongest 2025-era geometric method and the right target to land the
negative result against, at a realized-parameter-count matched budget.
\emph{(ii) Eval-matched calibration arms} would settle whether the data-aware
inits' residual penalty is geometry or a calibration artifact---if they return to
the curve, the cleaner result is ``the law is method-free once calibration is
fair.'' \emph{(iii) A gated-magnitude adapter}---an input-conditional gate scaling
only the adapter delta, trained against a preservation corpus disjoint from the
evaluation set---is the constructive follow-up, now with the higher bar of having
to beat LoRA{+}wd rather than the elaborate adapters.

The message for a field that ships a new adapter every week is a wake-up call:
\textbf{control the size of the update first---geometry sets where on the magnitude
curve a method starts, not a separate axis of forgetting.}

\section{Reproducibility and Compute}\label{app:repro}
% ===========================================================================
Because our method \emph{is} a sweep, the run count is part of the claim. The
complete Llama-2-7B commonsense evidence is the $8\times7$ learning-rate grid = 56 cells, of
which 49 are clean (CorDA's 7 cells withheld, Section~\ref{sec:limits}); the math
grid adds a sparse set (14 pooled cells across LoRA / LoRA{+}wd / DoRA), and the
Qwen2.5-7B replication adds $\sim$13 of $\sim$112 planned cells. Each cell is a full
fine-tune followed by in-process evaluation on the retention and adaptation suites.
All numbers are computed from a single cleaned registry
(\texttt{campaign\_summary\_clean.jsonl}), which spans every campaign---both
domains, both models, and auxiliary sweeps (e.g.\ the rank sweep)---and contains
\textbf{320 clean rows} after deduplication (359 raw rows $\to$ 343 unique by latest
\texttt{evaluated\_at}, then the residual-save explosion rows and withheld CorDA
cells removed); the raw registry is retained, labeled superseded, for provenance.
The 320-row total is larger than the 49+14+13 headline cells because it includes the
auxiliary and superseded-but-provenanced runs.

\paragraph{Estimator and registry mapping.} The magnitude axis of
Equation~\ref{eq:fdelta} is the registry field \texttt{fdelta}, computed
in-process at evaluation time by forward pre-hooks on every adapted module (100
prompts drawn evenly from the seven commonsense evaluation sets, batch 8,
max length 256; tokens with $\lVert x\rVert_2 \le 10^{-6}$ masked). The standalone
estimator additionally logs \texttt{fdelta\_matrix\_mean} (the per-matrix-mean
variant of the same statistic) and the spectral measures
\texttt{dw\_sv\_mean}/\texttt{dw\_sv\_max} compared in Figure~\ref{fig:axis}. For
residual-initialized adapters (MiLoRA, SC-LoRA, CorDA), $\Delta W_\ell$ includes
the initialization residual via a rank-$2r$ conversion at save time, so a $0$-step
run measures $\Delta W = 0$ (self-check passed for all released arms).

\paragraph{Companion experiments.} Two observations cited in
Section~\ref{sec:law} (the LoRA rank sweep and the weight-SVD leakage-direction
result $r\!\approx\!-0.09$) come from companion experiments that predate the
released registry; they are retained in the project's experiment archive, are not
recomputable from \texttt{campaign\_summary\_clean.jsonl}, and are used only as
consistency checks, never as headline numbers. The modest residual rank effect in
observation~(i) (partial correlation $-0.56$ at matched $\dw$) is instead computed
from the 320 saved adapter weights via an SVD-alignment drift battery; the same
battery recovers each method's initialization design from its \emph{trained} adapter
(e.g.\ MiLoRA is the only arm whose update energy leans toward the minor singular
subspace), which we use as a fingerprinting diagnostic---not as a competing
predictive axis, since direction in the weight-SVD basis is uninformative for
retention (Section~\ref{sec:law}, observation~(ii)).

\paragraph{Efficiency.} The practical corollary of Section~\ref{sec:pareto} extends
to compute. LoRA{+}wd requires no initialization pass and trains in wall-clock
indistinguishable from plain LoRA (a $\sim\!0.1\%$ difference in our logs), because
weight decay is a decoupled AdamW flag rather than a structural change. The other
arms pay strictly more for the same trainable-parameter budget. DoRA is
$\approx\!2.1\times$ slower---its magnitude--direction decomposition runs every
step---for no retention or adaptation benefit. The data-aware inits (MiLoRA, PiSSA,
LoRA-Null, SC-LoRA, CorDA) pay a one-time initialization tax---weight SVDs
(MiLoRA/PiSSA) or calibration forward passes plus an eigendecomposition or damped
inverse (LoRA-Null/SC-LoRA/CorDA), CorDA++ the most expensive at
$\approx\!5\times$ CorDA---after which they train as vanilla LoRA. CLoRA pays at
train time: it holds a frozen orthogonality-projection block resident in memory whose
size scales with $k$ ($0.42$\,GB at $k{=}128$, $1.67$\,GB at the default $k{=}512$,
$6.7$\,GB at $k{=}2048$), plus a $\sim\!10\%$ per-step overhead. On the retention
frontier, then, the subspace-free magnitude knob is also the cheapest.

\paragraph{Compute.} All runs used a single 8-GPU NVIDIA B200 node with one
training-plus-evaluation cell per GPU. From the scheduler's occupancy over the
campaign ($\sim$9 days of wall-clock for $\sim$288 cells across both models and
domains, plus documented losses to scheduler collisions and retry storms), we
estimate the total at \textbf{$\sim$1{,}700--2{,}000 B200-GPU-hours} (estimate
derived from cell counts $\times$ per-cell times, not metered).
